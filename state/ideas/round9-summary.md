## Dropped at the tool search (candidate, evidence, reason)
1. Self-storage dynamic pricing: Price Monster (quote only) already sells it, and management suites (Storable and others) bundle pricing; UK suites list at £0.45 to £0.75 per unit a month (SourceForge, Feb 2026).
2. Padel and sports court dynamic pricing: Playbypoint, Anolla and Koalendar list peak and off-peak or demand pricing inside the booking system.
3. Theatre and arts venue dynamic pricing: Spektrix has built-in bulk price changes and partners with TRG Arts DynamO (no upfront fee), which is also built for Tessitura.
4. Accountancy practice fee pricing: Ignition AI Price Insights (Oct 2025) and Infinite's pricing engine already exist.
5. UK food VAT classification: VATCalc already markets AI item classification; the HMRC notice is public.
6. R&D tax claim eligibility: HMRC's own free checker, ForrestBrown Claim Clarity (independent claim review), and no adviser fee data found. HMRC caseworkers also use Copilot.
7. Director salary and dividend optimiser: free calculators (RR Accountants), a £29 spreadsheet (AMF), and bundles in Crunch (£25 + VAT add-on), Gorilla and Accounts-OS.
8. Bid or no-bid for small contractors: BidPilot, ConstructionKit, Stotles qualification scoring, Tenderforge and BidScout already exist; overlaps the parked council-tender idea.
9. Nursery fee and funded-hours pricing: Ovivio free funding calculator, Famly pricing guidance, council planning tools. No consultant price found.
10. Care home fee uplift negotiation: councils use iESE Care Cubed for cost of care; no consultant price found.
11. SFI26 action selection: AHDB's SFI cost/benefit tool and the RPA limited-area calculator are free; windows 1 and 2 have closed (the second opened 22 September 2026).
12. PPC audit and management: 10% to 25% of spend or £300 to £2,000 a month, but audits are often free and Google's own tooling and many agencies cover it.
13. Plant and tool hire rate setting: MCS, Wynne RentalMan, Access and Abax are the only software found; no rate-recommendation product and no human price found, so Pay is not evidenced. Not pursued.

## Lessons for the validator
- Judgement outputs do have thinner competition than extraction (both survivors had no direct competitor on a first search), but the same search shows why: the buyer's price for the human decision is small (£250 per review, a fractional FD day) and the work is occasional. Expect Pay to be the lowest score.
- Where the output is a price, the mature verticals (storage, courts, theatres, hotels, accountancy) already sell it inside the booking or practice system. Look for verticals with no system of record, which is also why the data for the AI is missing.
- The rent idea is the better of the two: forcing function (Renters' Rights Act, tribunal decisions up about four times year on year), per-tenancy recurring work, a demo from open data. The data red flag (listings) is the test to run first.
- Suggested cheap kill tests: for rent, check whether Zoopla, Reapit, Alto or Street ship a rent suggestion (not checked this round); for food, repeat the CPI tool search and check Foodware 365 and similar ERP costing modules.
