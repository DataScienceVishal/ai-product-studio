# T-171 scout round: buyer-side priced jobs (bureau, agency or freelancer fee per unit)

Run date: 2026-11-27 (all sources accessed that day). Thesis: SMEs already outsource a messy-content job at a published per-unit or per-month fee; AI does most of it; a two-person company undercuts the bureau.

Result: 15 candidates tested, 0 written. No G0 file created. No RANKING.md or BOARD.md edits, no commits, no contact, no sign-ups. No prompt-injection attempts seen.

Method limit: standard-mode searches only (about 16 queries, no page fetches). Every price and incumbent line below is a search-result summary, not a fetched vendor page, and is marked as such. Most bureau prices come from vendor or comparison blogs. A drop on "AI incumbents exist" would still need the vendor page read before it were final, but in every case below the kill rests on several independent vendors, so the verdict is unlikely to flip.

## Lessons

- The thesis fails on the same wall as every earlier round, with a new twist: a bureau price list is easy to find, but the cheaper the bureau, the more certain it is that an AI vendor already undercuts it. Where the bureau fee is low (floorplans under £10, invoices £1.50-£5, payslips £1-£10), the job is already commoditised. Where the fee is high (CE files, SDS, ESG consultants), the buyer pays for liability and curated data, which a two-person AI company can't take on.
- The property inventory job had the clearest published per-unit price (£80-£145 per 2-bed) and a messy-photo input, but it is also the most contested: at least six AI inventory tools are aimed at the clerks and agents who would be the buyers. A new product there needs a wedge nobody has shown. Not found in this round.
- Pay evidence is easy to find; a wedge is not. The pre-screen needs a candidate where the incumbent check comes back empty, and 15 for 15 came back non-empty or non-recurring (annual, per product, or episodic).
- Jobs that are annual, one-off per product or episodic (VAT refunds, CE files, Amazon appeals, accounts filing) fail the subscription test even when the bureau fee is published.
- Candidates 9, 10 and 5 are the only ones with no AI incumbent found, and each had a thin search (one query). They fail for other reasons (no Pay, liability, episodic), so a deeper incumbent search would not change the verdict.
- Recommendation: report 0 honestly. A further desk round on this thesis is unlikely to pay; the remaining lever is buyer evidence, which is locked until `unlock selling`.
