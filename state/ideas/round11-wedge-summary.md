## 1. pepr-packaging-data-pipeline (68)

- Incumbent that sank it: Repackd, a self-serve UK tool. Own pricing page (fetched 2026-11-24, https://www.repackd.com/pricing): Starter £299/mo (500 SKUs, "small producers getting compliant", no document extraction), Growth £799/mo (5,000 SKUs, extraction, "mid-market producers"), Enterprise £1,999/mo. Annual billing gives 2 months free. Every plan has 1 user and unlimited RPD CSV export. Second anchor: Clarity RAM Assess "from £4,995 per annum" ex VAT (earlier validator read of Clarity's guide PDF, https://clarity.eco/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/RAM-Assess-Guide-August-2026.pdf).
- Candidate wedges tested:
  - Below Repackd Starter (small producers, 25 to 50 tonnes): they pay no disposal fees (https://www.gov.uk/guidance/epr-for-packaging-what-you-must-do-as-a-small-producer), so there is little to save and Pay falls. Repackd already targets them. Rejected.
  - Scheme-led resale (smaller schemes without a RAM tool): the scheme register (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/extended-producer-responsibility-for-packaging-register-of-packaging-compliance-schemes) lists the schemes, but the Excel files were not opened, so the count of schemes lacking a tool is unknown. No evidence any scheme wants to license a tool.
  - Direct registrants: no public count. The only figure is 344 old-regime direct registrants (EA 2025 compliance report, already in the file). Too small and unsized.
- Size evidence: a search for any public count of large versus small producers returned none (search 2026-11-24). The ~5,250 EA-registered figure has no size or route split.
- Rubric lines that move: none confirmed. Competition stays 3 (could drop to 2). Pay stays 14, since Repackd's £7,990 a year at Growth adds a second software price but also shows a cheaper rival exists. AI 10 and Build 11 are possible only after the T-14 benchmark (+2 total), but Competition still blocks it.
- Estimate: 66 to 68. Method: file's own "with items 1, 3, 5" path gave 73 only with Competition 5, and that is now disproved by Repackd.
- Recommendation: leave parked. Do not spend the second G1 attempt.

## 2. restaurant-margin-copilot (66)

- Incumbent that sank it: Jelly. Own pricing page (fetched 2026-11-24, https://getjelly.co.uk/pricing): £129 per location per month, one-off setup fee per site (amount not stated), monthly plan cancel anytime, annual 10% off, no invoice cap beyond "fair usage". Target is "restaurant and kitchen operators". The page mentions Xero integration but no accountant or partner programme. Also MarketMan Starter $249/mo "for independent operators" (https://marketman.com/pricing, from the earlier T-9 pass).
- Candidate wedges tested:
  - Accountant-led resale: Jelly's page shows no partner programme, so the channel is open on that page. But I found no evidence that hospitality accountants resell margin tools, or that independents buy through them. Sizing is generic only: about 39,975 accountancy enterprises (Rapport Digital analysis of ONS UK Business Counts, search summary, https://onrec.com/news/research-labour-market/uk-accountancy-firm-numbers-fall-86-as-sector-faces-succession-questions), most not hospitality-focused.
  - Much cheaper single-job tier: Profito (unverified) starts at €34 and KitchenIQ at $89 (T-9 pass), so £30 to £50 is not below the cheap end.
- Size evidence: about 28,955 café and unlicensed restaurant enterprises, ONS UK Business Counts March 2026 via a third-party analysis (search summary, https://www.ons.gov.uk/explore-local-statistics/indicators/food-outlets). ONS's restaurant size-band spreadsheet exists (https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/adhocs/3495foodservicesbyemploymentsizeuk2023to2025) but its figures are not on the page. The 1 to 3 site split is not public in what I could read.
- Rubric lines that move: Reach 7 to 8 at best (an unproven accountant channel). Nothing else. Competition stays 3. Pain stays 13 because the evidence is still about chains and vendors.
- Estimate: 65 to 66.
- Recommendation: leave parked. A kill candidate if nothing changes, since the T-9 pass already found no surviving gap.

## 3. holiday-pay-payroll-lookback-audit (63)

- Incumbents, from the validator's own-page checks: BrightPay says holiday pay "must be entered manually by the user" (brightpay.co.uk docs 25-26); Xero says a 52-week report "is not a feature we have planned" (productideas.xero.com, 27 Nov 2025); Moneysoft and Sage 50 do not calculate it automatically (search summaries). Forward-only calculation exists in Staffology, IRIS and Employment Hero (prices not published). The nearest "price" is human work: DCK Payroll Solutions charges holiday pay calculations as an extra at £73.50 per hour ex VAT (PDF read, https://greatandlittlehampdenparishcouncil.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/DCK-Fee-List-2025-26.pdf); Irwin Mitchell £1,500 + VAT per review. No incumbent "sank" it on features. What held it down was FWA timing, the planned free FWA calculator, and the Risk score.
- Wedge: sell to bureaux and accountants that run BrightPay, Xero, Moneysoft or Sage 50 (the packages that do not calculate), as a per-client holiday pay calculator plus history check. This is a different buyer (the bureau, not the employer) and a resale channel. It is also recurring, not a one-off audit.
- Size evidence (partial):
  - 1.24 million people on zero-hours contracts in March 2026 (Work Foundation analysis of ONS Labour Force Survey, search summary, https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/work-foundation/news/record-124-million-people-on-zero-hours-contracts-as-workers-report-work-life-chaos). This is a worker count, not a bureau count.
  - 39,975 UK accounting, bookkeeping, auditing and tax enterprises (Rapport Digital reading of ONS data, search summary, link above). No public SIC 69201 payroll-bureau count was found.
  - Employer-side: RSM survey, 11% calculate holiday pay by hand (validator file, sample size not stated).
  - So the segment is pointed at but not sized. The bureau count needs a Nomis query (public, no scraping), which I could not run in the time box.
- What it spends today: £73.50 per hour as a bureau extra (one price list) and a second list charging "price on application". Two substitute costs, no software price.
- Rubric lines that move, with the evidence needed:
  - Pay 12 to 13: bureau price lists already charge extra (found). To 14 needs a published software price.
  - Risk 5 to 7: only through the pseudonymised design the validator specified. That is a design commitment, not new evidence, so the security agent must sign it off.
  - AI 7 to 8 and Build 11 to 12: only after the benchmark on public report layouts. Armstrong Watson (July 2026) says leave data sits outside payroll, which pulls Build down.
  - Pain 15 to 16: needs the government's consultation response (not published as of the last check) or bureau job ads naming 52-week or 12.07% work (none found).
  - Competition 6: no change. Fair Work Solutions (NMW platform, fetched by the validator 2026-11-24) and the planned FWA calculator are the kill-test risks.
- Estimate: 65 to 68. Method: validator's own path (items 2 to 5 give 70) with Pay at 13 not 14 per the scout's T-23 view, so 69 only if every other item lands; I weight that low.
- Recommendation: leave parked. This is the one worth a re-score, but only after (1) the government response is published and (2) the FWA calculator scope is known. Both are external, so there is nothing to do in the meantime except a Nomis payroll-bureau count.

- Rubric lines that move: none. Competition stays 1 to 2. Pain and Pay are unchanged.
- Estimate: 59 to 61. The validator already said the score tops out in the mid 60s even with all three items.
- Recommendation: leave parked. Kill candidate.

## 5. subcontractor-rams-drafting (59)

- Incumbents that sank it: at least ten tools. RAMSGen £59, £99 and £199 a month, drafts from the uploaded pre-construction pack (https://www.ramsgen.com/); swiftRMS £49 a month unlimited (https://swiftrms.co.uk/compare/rams-software-uk); RAMS AI £39.97 a month (https://ramsai.co.uk/); RamsBox £7 per RAMS (search summary); HandsHQ from £275 a month for up to 10 users (https://handshq.com/pricing); ECA eRAMS free to ECA members.
- Wedges tested:
  - Trade segment the tools ignore: none found. The tools are trade-agnostic and RAMSGen offers custom templates, which covers principal-contractor formats.
  - Accountant-led resale: not relevant. Trade bodies are the natural adviser channel, and ECA already gives electricians a free tool.
  - Cheaper tier: the cost floor is already £7 a document.
- Size evidence: the segment is large but already served. DBT business population estimates for 2026: about 846,000 construction SMEs, 15% of all SMEs (fetched 2026-11-24, https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/business-population-estimates-2026/business-population-estimates-for-the-uk-and-regions-2026-statistical-release). Specialised-trade enterprise count by size band is in an ONS download I did not open (https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/adhocs/2900enterprisesinspecialisedconstruction2022to2024). Size does not help when the price floor is £7.
- Rubric lines that move: none. Competition stays 1. Risk stays 6 (safety-critical).
- Estimate: 57 to 59.
- Recommendation: leave parked. Kill candidate.

## Cross-cutting note

- Three of the five have no wedge, and the other two are blocked by a named incumbent (Repackd) or by external timing (FWA). This supports the round's premise: price and channel wedges do not fix an incumbent that already sells the same job at a lower price.
- Cheapest next step with any value: a Nomis UK Business Counts pull for SIC 69201 and for 56.101/56.102 by size band. Both are public data.
