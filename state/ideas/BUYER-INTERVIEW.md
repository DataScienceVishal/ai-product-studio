# Buyer interview sheet (15 minutes)

For Vishal, in a chat with his friend, and optionally 2-3 of the friend's business contacts (introduced by the friend). Why: 17 desk-only rounds found nothing at 70+. A real conversation is the one signal other builders do not have (see META-REVIEW-strategist.md, option a).

## Ground rules (Mom Test)
- Ask about the past ("last time", "how much", "who"), not the future ("would you buy").
- Do not mention AI, the studio's products or prices. Do not pitch.
- Do not suggest answers. If they give an opinion, ask for the last time it happened.
- Silence is fine. Ask "what else?" before moving on.
- Ask permission to take notes. Company-level facts only: no names of staff, customers or other individuals.

## Questions (in order)
1. What does your business do, roughly how many people, and who decides what gets spent on tools or help?
2. What is the most tedious or repetitive admin job your team does every week or month? Tell me about the last time.
3. How long does it take, who does it, and how often? What happens when it is late or goes wrong?
4. What do you use for it today: a tool, a spreadsheet, an agency, an accountant, a hire? What does that cost per month?
5. What have you tried to fix it, and why did you stop or switch?
6. Where do you lose money or customers because something was missed, slow or chased too late?
7. When you last bought software or outside help, who signed it off, how long did it take, and what price felt easy versus painful?
8. (Friend as channel) Which problems do your own customers or business contacts keep raising with you, and which ones have they paid someone to solve? Who else would be worth a 15-minute chat?

## What to write down (box)
- Hours per week or month, and how many people do it
- £ per month: tools, agency, staff time, lost revenue
- Tools and suppliers named (company names only)
- How often the job happens, and any deadline
- Who signs off spending, and the usual budget size
- What they tried and why it failed
- Their exact words for the pain (only if they agree; never invent or paraphrase as a quote)

## What the studio does with the answers
Each pain with money attached becomes an idea file in `state/ideas/`, tagged "buyer-sourced". It is scored with the rubric and, if it lands at 63-69, goes through the proof-test route in `playbooks/pipeline.md` (kill test written first). Pains with no money attached are logged, not built. Nothing is promised or priced to anyone; selling stays locked until Vishal sends `unlock selling`.

## Privacy
Record business-level facts only. No personal data about individuals; no names of staff, customers or contacts in the repo. Contact is made by Vishal, not by the studio.
