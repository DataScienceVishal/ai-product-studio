3. **R&D tax relief pre-submission checks (AIF).** Dead on free substitutes and shrinking demand. ForrestBrown's Claim Clarity is a free independent review of a company's latest claim in about two weeks (https://www.adsgroup.org.uk/knowledge/industry-member-news/forrestbrown-launches-claim-clarity-for-r-d-tax-relief/); HMRC publishes its own free "is it R&D" tool (https://www.icaew.com/insights/tax-news/2025/oct-2025/new-hmrc-tool-for-r-and-d-tax-relief-claims); BDO sells a claim benchmarking and risk toolkit (https://store.bdo.co.uk/product/r-and-d-benchmarking-toolkit); advance clearances pilot exists. Annual per claim, the AIF is a fixed form (partly deterministic), and a wrong "qualifying R&D" call is the claimant's liability. Blick Rothenberg warns of human-oversight needs for AI-drafted claims (opinion). Not repeatable monthly work.

Sedric publishes a template page (vendor content). Dropped: the job is annual (round 9 lesson), no price for it, no data source for a proof test (the value is in the firm's own monitoring data), and Sedric and Adclear already pitch Consumer Duty.

## Lessons for the validator and next scout
- "Second-checker" is the natural place for incumbents in 2026: Xero (Syntax, free), IRIS, Adclear and Sedric already sell AI first-pass review with human sign-off. An AI that cites the rulebook is a standard feature, not a gap. Check the practitioner-built or free tools first (Syntax, Claim Clarity).
- The pricing hole recurs: consultancy fees are "on request" or tied to full preparation, so the price of the checking job itself stays unproven. Needs a buyer question, not a desk search.
- Regulated-firm work is usually personal or confidential data (client files, board MI), so proof tests on synthetic data show format only.
- AML is the one survivor-in-waiting, because the FCA takeover is a forcing function and no AI "independent file review" product was found, but the evidence bar was not met. Retry only with readable supervisor reports (fail rates, fines) and a priced independent-review quote.
- Recommendation: agrees with rounds 18 and 19. Further desk rounds on Vishal's edges have a low yield; buyer-side evidence is the better use of time.
