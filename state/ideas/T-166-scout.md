## Lessons

- The brief's premise, that a dated rule creates a recurring job where AI is essential, failed on both halves in every case. Where the job is recurring and dated (digital waste tracking, DMCC reminders, guaranteed hours), the data is already structured, so the core is a calculation or an API call and AI is decorative. Where the inputs are messy documents (right-to-work, certificates, DSARs), the job is rare, personal-data heavy, or already sold.
- Digital waste tracking is the best forcing-function find (real date, per-load frequency), and it is killed by market structure: Defra itself lists about 300 compatible providers. Check the regulator's own supplier list before scoring Competition.
- Vertical CRM vendors close "AI reads compliance documents" in a quarter: Alto shipped AutoCert, and Reapit shipped an RRA readiness tracker. Pre-empt this by searching the CRM vendors' blogs first.
- The ERA 2025 commencement dates keep moving (the gov.uk timeline was updated 22 Oct and 25 Nov 2026), and the details that matter (guaranteed-hours reference period, NDA regulations) are not final. Any ERA idea needs a final SI to build against; none exists yet for guaranteed hours.
- HR incumbents (Breathe, BrightHR, Brightmine) give away templates for new duties within months, so a duty with a checklist answer gets no Pay.
- Retrofit evidence is the one unfilled gap (no AI photo checker found), but the schemes end in 2027 and there is no installer-side price or volume. Next step would be a buyer question, which is locked.
- Recommendation: stop the "UK regulation to recurring SME job" thesis. It has now produced about 20 killed or dropped ideas across rounds 3 to 21. The next scout should start from a buyer-side priced job (agency or bureau fees per unit), or report 0 honestly.
