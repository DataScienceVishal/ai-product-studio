## WATCH: candidate 3, UK R&D relief technical narrative (not a KEEP)

Why it is the nearest: it is the only candidate where (a) the work is judgement from messy evidence (classifying which projects resolve scientific or technological uncertainty, writing the narrative for HMRC's additional information form), (b) AI is plausibly essential, (c) the human price is a percentage of the credit (Claimer 5%, capped GBP 10,000, in 2020), which is a large per-claim fee, and (d) it fits Vishal's tax edge. The only AI-first rival found is Taxnova (pre-seed, no price found), so the field is thin.

Why it is not a KEEP:
- No new 2026-27 rule was found. It is an old regime, so there is no forcing function.
- Claims are annual per company, so recurring revenue is weak.
- Advisers and HMRC treat AI-written narratives as a risk, and the narrative must reflect the firm's own human problem-solving, so a product that drafts it may carry sign-off liability.
- The demand test needs real project evidence (tickets, commits), which is hard to get and hard to demo. Synthetic repositories could show format but not whether HMRC would accept the claim.
- The outcome of a claim is a tax position, which is near the line of advice.

First spot-check for a validator (about 20 minutes): fetch Taxnova's own site for its price and feature list, then search the UK R&D claim-prep market (ABGI, Leyton, small boutiques) for published fees and the share of enquiries opened on narrative weakness (HMRC statistics). If Taxnova's price is under about GBP 200 a month or it already outputs the additional information form text, drop it. If it is priced like a consultancy and no SME tool exists, ask whether HMRC's enquiry rate data supports a "defensibility check" product (scoring a draft claim's narrative against CIRD81910 criteria) rather than a drafting product.

## Lessons for the next scout

- Twelve rule-driven niches in a row found a vendor with a visible price within one search. For SME compliance work in late 2026, search "<rule> AI" before anything else, because a small vendor appears within months of the date being set.
- Several rules are final but low-cost for the buyer: Ofcom (free toolkit), EHRC (free guidance), IASME (assessor fee about GBP 210 to 300). The regulator's free tool sets the price ceiling.
- Judgement work that sits next to advice (harassment duty, SAR exemptions, R&D eligibility) is cut by the off-limits line as well as by Competition.
- The drinks DRS and the CLP reclassification are the only two with a hard 2027-28 date and no verified AI price for the exact job (DRS), but the DRS job is per-SKU data collection like pEPR. Re-check DRS after the DMO publishes its fee framework and portal specification.
- Not tested this round and worth a search next: guaranteed-hours offers (still waiting on the government response, see T-143 candidate 5), and the holiday-pay look-back (rank 1, parked).
