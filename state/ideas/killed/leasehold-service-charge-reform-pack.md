# Leasehold service-charge reform pack

- slug: leasehold-service-charge-reform-pack
- stage: KILLED (G0, first G1 attempt, T-144, 2026-11-26)
- market and segment: England residential leasehold. Independent managing agents (about 5 to 200 blocks) and self-managed RTM and RMC companies. Social landlords follow from 1 Nov 2028.
- one-line pitch: builds each block's budget, prescribed-format service charge demand, standard statement of account and annual report from its invoices and ledger, ready for the accountant's ISRS 4400 report.

## Problem and who has it

The Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 (Part 4) makes landlords and managing agents use prescribed forms. From 1 Apr 2027 there are future-demand notices and new information-request rights. From 1 Nov 2027 there are prescribed demands, a standard written statement of account, an annual report and an administration-charge schedule, for private landlords. A demand that isn't in the prescribed form disapplies the lease's non-payment and late-payment provisions until a compliant demand is served. The buyers are agents and RTM or RMC directors who issue demands at least twice a year per block and produce year-end accounts once a year.

## Why AI is essential (and the moat)

It isn't. The output is a prescribed form filled from ledger totals, so it is deterministic. The AI parts are invoice-to-block and service charge head coding, which Inox already sells, and the annual report narrative. The annual report needs facts about building condition and planned works from a surveyor or agent, so the model can't supply them. No domain data or evaluation moat is visible.

## Rough build plan and Azure cost

A ledger and invoice CSV importer, a head-coding step on a cheap model, Schedule 2-4 form renderers, a budget-against-last-year table, and an accountant hand-off pack. Azure cost would be small, under $10 for a demo on synthetic blocks. The hard part is that agents' ledgers live in Qube, Blockman, Resident, PropLinker and Inox, none of which has a documented public API in the sources checked. A standalone product means entering the data twice.

## Risks, and what would prove this wrong

- Risk: a wrong demand makes charges unrecoverable until it is reissued, so the liability is real. Deciding what is recoverable "under the lease" is lease interpretation. The product has to stay a form-preparer, not a judge of recoverability, to avoid becoming a legal-advice product. Leaseholder names and arrears are personal data, but at a modest scale.
- What would have proved it right: incumbents publicly declining or lagging on the 2027 formats, a published per-block fee for the compliance work, or a segment (for example RTM companies on spreadsheets) with a measured time cost and no tool. None was found. Resident's commitment points the other way.

## Kill reason

The scout's main premise was wrong. The scout said the prescribed forms weren't final, so all vendors were "on the same start line". In fact SI 2026/1190 was made on 10 Nov 2026 with the demand, annual report and statement-of-account forms in Schedules 2-4, nearly a year before the 1 Nov 2027 start. Being on the same start line favours the incumbents: the form is public, it is a deterministic fill from ledger data the block software already holds, and at least one vendor (Resident) has publicly committed to ship the new demand, budget, reconciliation and annual report templates in its standard product. Trade bodies (Propertymark) tell agents to check their existing software. The small-block segment already has Ringley Service Charge Sorted (from £525 a year) and BlockKeeper (£100 per unit per year). The only AI-shaped piece, invoice-to-block coding, is already sold by Inox. Nobody publishes a separate price for the compliance work. The wedge collapses into a free feature of software buyers already pay for.

## Lessons

- A regulation with a fixed form is a template job for whoever holds the ledger. Before writing a G0 file on a "prescribed form" rule, find out who holds the data the form is filled from, and search "<vendor> + <rule> templates" on the vendors' own sites. Resident's page took one fetch.
- Check legislation.gov.uk for a made SI before writing "not yet published". Here the form was made 16 days before the scout's file.
- A vendor-bylined trade article (Flat Living, Inox) is marketing, not a pain signal.
- "A professional must sign it off" is a wedge for the professional, not for a software tool.

## What would revive it

All three would be needed: (1) evidence by mid-2027 that major block software (MRI Qube, Blockman, PropLinker, Resident) won't support Schedules 2-4, or supports them only as a paid module; (2) a published per-block price for preparing the statement of account or annual report, or for the ISRS 4400 report; (3) an AI-essential component with a measurable quality gap, such as drafting the annual report's works and condition section from the agent's documents, scored against a written rubric. Otherwise watch the social-landlord start (1 Nov 2028) only if housing-management vendors (MRI, NEC, Civica) are seen lagging.
