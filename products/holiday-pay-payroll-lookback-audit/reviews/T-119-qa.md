## Medium

### M1. Leave weeks that are not in leave.csv are silently ignored, so the "first 4 weeks" rule is mis-counted with no warning
Repro: worker A, Saturday weeks from 2024-06-01 (70 weeks), basic 400 every week, commission 60 on odd-numbered weeks. Weeks 60-63 (2025-07-26 .. 2025-08-16) carry only `A,<date>,holiday_pay,430.00` (four leave weeks already taken). Week 64 (2025-08-23) also carries `holiday_pay,430.00`. leave.csv lists only `A,2025-08-23`.
Command: `audit.py --history h.csv --leave l.csv --out out --threshold 0`
Actual: exit 0, "1 leave weeks checked, 0 exceptions". The week is treated as the first leave week of the leave year (normal pay, due 430 = paid 430).
Expected: it is the 5th leave week of the year, so basic-only pay applies (due 400, overpaid 30), or at minimum a warning that history holds `holiday_pay` rows for weeks not in leave.csv. The README says the first 4 weeks taken count as normal pay, but FORMAT.md never tells the user that leave.csv must list every leave week of the leave year, and nothing checks it.
Fix: in the loader, collect weeks with a `holiday_pay` row that are not in leave.csv and add them to `report.md` as a warning section ("holiday pay recorded but no leave row"). Better, count them toward the 4 in `used_in_year`. Add a line to FORMAT.md.

### M2. Leave week recorded as ordinary pay (no `holiday_pay` row) gives a false underpayment with no flag
Repro (dir `lb2`): 60 Saturday weeks from 2024-06-01 at `A,<d>,basic,400.00`, plus leave week 2025-07-26 as `A,2025-07-26,basic,400.00` (no holiday_pay row). leave.csv `A,2025-07-26`.
Actual: `A,2025-07-26,0.00,400.00,400.00,underpaid,,52,0,0,400.00,` with no flag. Paid is shown as 0.00 although £400 was paid in that week.
Expected: a flag such as `leave_week_has_pay_but_no_holiday_pay` and the week's other pay shown, because mapping leave to basic is very common in payroll exports (and the Part B mapper could produce it). A false claim is the worst failure for an audit tool.
Fix: when the leave week has rows but no `holiday_pay`, add the flag and the text "pay in this week was not marked as holiday pay: £X". Consider treating it as paid.

### M3. A leave week with no usable data is silently dropped from the exceptions, and the report reads as clean
Repro (dir `early`): history `W1,2025-06-07,basic,400.00` and `W1,2025-06-14,basic,400.00`; leave.csv `W1,2025-05-31` (leave before any history).
Actual: exit 0, "1 leave weeks checked, 0 exceptions", report.md "Exceptions: None." The only hint is "Workers with flags: 1 (W1)" in the summary. The flags are `no_reference_weeks` and `leave_week_missing_from_history`, due 0 and paid 0, so the week is not an exception unless `--include-flagged` is given. The same happens when the leave week is absent from history and there are no reference weeks.
Expected: weeks that could not be audited should be listed (or counted) as "not checked", not as clean.
Fix: always list rows carrying `no_reference_weeks` (or add the line "Leave weeks that could not be checked: N" to the summary and a section listing them). Note that when the leave week has paid rows and no history (variant `early2`), the output says "overpaid by £100" with due 0.00, which is a misleading label; show "cannot be checked" for `no_reference_weeks`.

## Low

### L1. Huge `--threshold` crashes with a traceback (exit 1)
Repro: `audit.py --history h.csv --leave l.csv --out out --threshold 1e27` (also 1e28, 1e30, 1e400).
Actual: `decimal.InvalidOperation: [<class 'decimal.InvalidOperation'>]`, exit 1 (the validators say exit 2 for bad input, argparse errors use 2).
Expected: "invalid threshold" and exit 2.
Fix: in `_threshold` reject values above, say, 1,000,000,000 and values whose `quantize(Decimal("0.01"))` raises.

### L2. Years near 0001 give a traceback; no date range check
Repro (dir `d0001`): history `W1,0001-01-08,basic,100` and `W1,0001-01-15,basic,100` (Mondays), leave `W1,0001-01-15`.
Actual: `ValueError: invalid leave year start date` from `engine/lookback.py:123`, exit 1. Extreme dates elsewhere "work" (`9999-12-31` gives a result, and a 0001 to 2025 history range gives a report with a range of about 105,000 missing weeks), which suggests a plausible-date check is missing (a typo like `0225-06-07` in a real file would pass `YYYY-MM-DD` validation).
Fix: in the loader, refuse dates before 2000-01-01 or more than about a year in the future, with a line-level message.

### L3. Usability in bulk errors, false positives and ignored settings
- A payday that moves by one day for part of the year (30 of 60 weeks) produces 30 separate lines, one per row. A 500-worker file with the wrong format produced one error per row (62 lines for 60 rows, shown as `uni`). Suggest a cap such as the first 50 issues plus "and N more", or one summary line per worker for the weekday problem.
- NI check false positive: pseudonymous payroll ID `EM100234A` (two letters, six digits, one letter) is refused as a National Insurance number (`ni` dir). Document that this ID shape is not allowed, as users commonly have IDs of this form.
- `contract_weekly_basic` supplied for a `variable_hours` worker is silently ignored (`fx4`), and `contract_weekly_basic` of `0` for a `fixed_pay` worker is accepted and gives due 0.00 with "overpaid" (`fx2`, flagged). Both deserve a validation error.
- Non-ASCII IDs (Hindi `कर्मचारी1`) are refused by design, but the message does not say that ASCII letters only are allowed. Add "ASCII" to the text.

## What I tried that worked (no bug)
- UTF-8 BOM (history, leave, workers), CRLF line endings, no trailing newline, trailing blank lines and `,,,` / whitespace-only rows: all load and give the same result as the plain file.
- Quoted amounts (`"400.00"`) accepted. Rows in reversed (out-of-order) order give identical output. A zero-amount row of an excluded category changes nothing.
- Rejected with a clear line-level message and exit 2, nothing written: UTF-16 file, header only, empty file, Excel dates (`01/06/2024`, `26/7/2025`), semicolon-separated file, wrong-case header, `1,400.00`, duplicate history rows, duplicate leave rows, leave for an unknown or different-case ID, leave `weeks` of 0, 1.5 and `.5`, `FIXED_PAY`, settings for a worker with no history, history that is a directory or a missing file.
- `--out` pointing at an existing file, an existing directory named exceptions.csv, an unwritable path and the empty string: clear message, exit 2. Output files were created mode 0600.
- Thresholds: 0 (any difference of a penny or more is listed), 0.001, 1e-400, 99.99 and 100 behave consistently at the boundary. `-1` and `NaN` exit 2 with a clean message.
- Huge amount `999999999.99`: no crash. Unicode IDs refused by design (see L3).
- Performance: 500 workers x 104 weeks (about 79,500 history rows, 1,500 leave weeks) ran in 1.06 s.
- Leave week 0.5 and 0.50: due is half the weekly rate and is compared with the holiday_pay row. Leaver whose history stops before the leave: flags `shortened;leave_week_missing_from_history`.
- Leave years: leave weeks ending 2025-03-29 and 2025-04-05 are attributed to the leave year of the week-ending date as documented. 2024 leap year data raised no errors.
- Fixed pay: contract basic 400 with commission 60 on 26 of 52 weeks gives due 430.00 (hand: 400 + 26 x 60 / 52 = 430.00). Blank contract basic gives the `rate_inferred_no_contract_basic` flag.

## Hand-checked figures (52-week rule)
1. Variable hours, Saturday weeks from 2023-07-01: basic 400.00 for weeks 0-69, 500.00 for weeks 70-74, leave week 75 (2024-12-07), holiday_pay 400.00. By hand the 52 weeks before are weeks 23-74: 47 x 400 + 5 x 500 = 21,300.00; 21,300 / 52 = 409.6153...; due 409.62, underpaid 9.62. Engine: due 409.62, difference 9.62 (`s1`). Match.
2. Six consecutive leave weeks in one leave year (2025-04-05 .. 2025-05-10); 60 prior weeks of basic 400 plus commission 100 in alternate weeks (average 450.00). By hand: weeks 1-4 normal pay 450.00, weeks 5 and 6 basic only 400.00. Holiday weeks are skipped from the average, so the average stays 450. With holiday_pay 450 paid every week, the engine lists only the last two weeks (2025-05-03 and 2025-05-10) as overpaid 50.00 each, with `ref_weeks_skipped` 4 and 5. Match (`s3`). With the same data shifted so that three weeks fall either side of 2025-04-01, all six are normal pay and no exception arises, as the leave-year rule says.
