## 3. Where a cheaper entrant could win

Honest verdict: a narrow gap, not a clear one.

- Lowest price for invoice-to-GP today. The cheapest tools that claim the full job are Profito (EUR 34, but 5 invoices a month, unverified) and KitchenIQ (USD 89, unverified). The cheapest verified, UK-facing, invoice-plus-recipe-plus-alert product is Jelly at GBP 129 per site plus a setup fee. Dext does invoice reading from about GBP 24 to 41 a month but does no recipe costing. Kitchen Porter Tech is USD 25 but its invoice reading is unconfirmed. So an entrant at GBP 39 to 69 would undercut every verified full-job tool by about 47 to 70%, but would not beat Profito's entry tier on price, and a 5-invoice cap makes that tier a poor fit for a real kitchen.
- Segments the leaders ignore (inferred from their own pages). MarketMan Starter and Supy are USD-priced and aimed at operators with budget (Supy "2 to 250 branches"; MarketMan Starter caps scans at 50 and email scanning costs extra). Apicbase starts at 5 outlets. Nory and Procure Wizard are quote-only and group-oriented. Jelly is the only one clearly aimed at a single UK site, and it ties its best margin features to a POS integration (pass-on fee from GBP 15). Possible openings: (a) a single site that has no integrated POS or a POS Jelly does not list; (b) owners who want a flat, small, no-setup-fee, cancel-anytime price; (c) CORRECTED 2026-11-24: invoices-first price alerts are NOT an opening. Jelly's homepage advertises daily notifications when a supplier moves a price, "Live price tracking" is on its pricing page, and per-ingredient tracking needs no recipes. Jelly also claims human-checked extraction, so we must match a checked rival on accuracy. Honest framing for (a) and (b): less product (no POS link, ordering or human check) for much less money, no setup fee. These are inferences, not proven demand.
- Not found on any own page seen. A written plain-English "what to do" weekly message (re-price, swap supplier, adjust portion). Jelly already offers margin warnings "actioned the same day", so this is a presentation layer it could add quickly; it is not a moat. Brakes, Bidfood and Booker are not named as integrations on any pricing page read.
- Public review complaints. Not verified this session: G2 and Capterra returned 403, and a Trustpilot search found nothing for Jelly (the results were other products named Jelly). The only complaints on file come from the earlier note (search snippets, 2026-11-23): MarketMan setup "very tedious", "weeks", and a G2 user saying invoice scanning "did not work 50% of the time". Nothing on Jelly's accuracy or price was found, and absence of evidence is not evidence of good accuracy.
- Threats to a cheap entrant. Free tools from wholesalers (Bidfood MyRecipes, Brakes Virtual Chef) cover single-supplier buyers. Dext already reads invoice lines at low cost. Jelly or Kitchen CUT's KC Lite could add a low tier. No evidence was found that UK independents would pay GBP 39 to 69 but not GBP 129, or how many buy from several suppliers.
