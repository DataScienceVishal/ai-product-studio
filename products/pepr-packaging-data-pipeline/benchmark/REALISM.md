# Realism check: synthetic benchmark vs real public supplier sheets (T-56)

Written 2026-11-24. All documents accessed 2026-11-24 from public URLs, no login, nobody contacted. Company-level product documents only. Page text was treated as data; no page contained instructions aimed at an AI reader (nothing to report on that front).

**Method and limits (read first).**
- I had no shell in this task. I opened each PDF through the fetch tool and the PDF viewer, and I read the rendered page plus the embedded text layer where the viewer showed one. I did NOT run `pypdf.extract_text()` on any of them, so every statement about what pypdf would return is an inference, marked "to test".
- Search was in English and only surfaced PDFs that search engines index. That biases the sample towards clean, born-digital, English sheets. Scanned and emailed supplier documents (the kind a producer actually holds) are not publicly indexed, so my sample cannot say how common they are. Treat "0 of 13 scanned" as a floor on the real rate, not an estimate.
- Only 2 of 13 are UK suppliers (Colorlites, KB Packaging). The rest are EU (3), US (5), AU (3). UK-specific practice (for example British Glass TEC 4 tolerances) is under-sampled.
- 13 documents is a spot check, not a statistical sample. Percentages below are counts out of 13 and should be read as "seen in N of 13".
- No document's licence or terms were found that clearly allow redistribution. Links only; nothing is copied into the repo.

Benchmark facts used for comparison come from `benchmark/README.md`, `taxonomy.py` and `generate.py` (6 templates: table, matrix, bom, prose, prose_buried, two_column; units g/kg/oz/mg; weights are single values; features come from closed vocabularies; 128 of 457 scored slots are missing; no recycled-content field; ram_category is derived from the material and is never printed on a sheet).

## 2. Patterns in real sheets that the generator does NOT cover, ranked by likely effect on accuracy

Ranking is my judgement of effect on the field-accuracy score of a real extractor, using how often it appeared and how many scored slots it touches.

1. **Unit weight is absent or given in a form that is not grams per unit.** Seen in 8 of 13: areal weight and thickness only (films, labels, paper: rows 5, 6, 10, 11, 12), case or roll weight only (rows 6, 8), and no weight at all (row 7). The generator always prints a per-component weight (maybe missing by marker, never replaced by a different quantity). On real sheets the weight slot is mostly "missing", and the main risk is a model deriving grams from gsm or inventing one. This is the biggest gap because weight_g is one of three core slots per component.
2. **Weights with tolerances, odd unit spellings and units kept outside the number.** "37.00gm ± 2.00gm" (rows 1, 2), "8.25 +/- 0.37 oz." (row 4), "1.38 oz (+/- 0.04 oz)" (row 9), and a bare "167" under a row label "Glass weight (g)" (row 3). The generator has only a stand-alone "Tolerance +/- 0.2 g" distractor and always writes the unit next to the number. Note a scorer interaction: `taxonomy.parse_weight` uses `\b` after the unit, so "37.00gm" does not parse as grams, and a quote that is only "167" has no unit, so `quote_ok` would reject it. Either would score a correct extraction as wrong or untraceable. This needs a dated amendment before any further run if such sheets are added.
3. **One document, many variants or many rows.** Pactiv lists 15 SKU rows, Plastotecnica has 8 thickness columns, Veritiv has 3 gauge columns, SAICA has one grade per page (15 pages). The generator has one product per document. The extractor on a real sheet must pick the intended variant; our schema and truth have no way to say which. High impact on component matching.
4. **Single-component sheets where most features are "not stated", plus non-answers.** A bottle sheet says "Various 24/410 Vision stock closure options" (rows 1, 2); a tray sheet lists "Secondary packaging: MDPE liner, brown cardboard box, pallet" with no weights (rows 7, 9). The generator's feature rows are always either a vocabulary phrase or a marker (`n/a`, `TBC`...). Real sheets give non-committal text and add secondary packaging that might be wrongly returned as extra components. Medium-high effect on feature and component slots. (Real sheets do use `n/a`, `TBC`, `NIL`, blank cells, so the missing-marker set itself is realistic.)
5. **Recycled content and blends inside the material field.** "rPET ... (PCR100%)", "rPET 30% + PET 70%" on two lines for one bottle, "minimum of 25% PCR", "100% Kraft Paper" under Recycled Content. The closed vocabulary has `PET` and `rPET` as separate values and no recycled-content slot; a 30/70 blend has no single correct answer. Medium effect on the material slot.
6. **Table layout that breaks reading order.** Vision's text layer is all labels first, then all values (viewer text layer; pypdf to test). Multi-column spec tables with units in headers (Plastotecnica, Veritiv, Avery) are also common. The generator's PDFs are drawn row by row, so pypdf text keeps label and value together. Medium effect on text-fed extractors, small on an extractor that gets page images.
7. **Colour stated in odd places or not at all.** "Finish: Clear" (row 3, the word "Colour" does not appear), "WhiteSolid" with "Colour ID: W1229" (row 1), "Variable Coloured" (row 9), "Coloured" as a tick-box option (row 6), colour missing (rows 5, 7, 12), and "Black" with no mention of pigment (row 8). The generator's labels are Shade, Tint, Colour and values come from a closed list; carbon black is always printed explicitly. Medium effect on the colour slot, and the carbon-black rule (8 of 68 components) would almost never be triggerable on real sheets.
8. **Marketing recyclability claims, resin codes and certification icons.** "100% recyclable" (rows 6, 12), "Material recyclable? Yes" (row 3), "Recyclable*" with an asterisk (row 8), "SPI recycle code 4" in prose (row 5), "4 LDPE (according to 129/97/EC)" (row 6), logos for FSC, RecyClass, Kiwa, UNE as images (rows 11, 13). None of these is a RAM class, and ram_category in our benchmark is derived from the material, so the score is barely touched. They matter for the product (what evidence is stored) but little for this kill test.
9. **Non-English text and mixed-language pages.** Uline pp 3-6 are Spanish and French, SAICA mixes Spanish and English. The generator is English only. Low to medium on this sample (my searches were in English, so this is likely understated).
10. **Encrypted or print-to-PDF files.** Uline's PDF uses AES-128 encryption (opens without a password); Avery 79059 is a browser print of a 2013 web page with URL and timestamp footers. pypdf needs the `cryptography` package to read AES-encrypted files (to test). Low effect on accuracy, but a real failure mode for the pipeline.
11. **Scans and photos.** Not found among public files (see limits), but real producers hold scanned or photographed sheets. Our 5 raster sheets cover this; I cannot say whether the skew-and-noise recipe resembles real scans.

## 3. Patterns the benchmark has that real sheets rarely show

- **Conflicting weights inside one document** (table says 24 g, a footnote or packing note says 26 g): 0 of 13. Real inconsistencies are more often between documents or versions than inside one sheet. The 22 conflict slots (and the 80% conflict bar) test something real sheets rarely exercise. Makes the benchmark HARDER on that bar, and gives unrealistic weight to conflict handling in the overall 457-slot score.
- **Per-component weights for 2-4 components in one document** (matrix, BOM, two-column templates): 0 of 13 sheets listed weights for more than one component; real sheets describe one item, and the bill of materials lives in a separate file or system. Probably EASIER on component matching (names are given with weights) but unrealistic.
- **Mixed units within one sheet** (g, kg, oz, mg together): 0 of 13. Real sheets stay in one system, metric or "American Customary Units" (row 9), and never mg. HARDER than reality on unit conversion, but real sheets bring tolerance and unit-spelling problems the generator lacks.
- **Features stated explicitly as closed-vocabulary phrases** (closure with liner, label type, sleeve, adhesive, ink, barrier): in 13 real documents, closure, label, sleeve, ink and barrier were stated in none. Adhesive appeared on label stock only (rows 10, 11, 12). So real sheets would score a high missing rate, which is EASIER (return `missing`) unless the model over-reaches, and the 0.45 to 0.6 feature-present rate in the generator is far above reality.
- **Buried prose** (values hidden in paragraphs with filler): real prose appears (Duni, Veritiv highlights) but never carries the weight or material; real sheets are overwhelmingly key-value tables. HARDER than reality for the buried template (S021, S027).
- **Short distractor list that is always numeric weights** (gross weight, pallet weight, case count): real distractors are broader (temperature ranges, regulation numbers, MVTR, tensile, brimful capacity, case cube) but not usually number-plus-weight-unit lookalikes. Pactiv's "Gross case weight (lbs.)" is the one real close lookalike.
- **Clean tier**: labels, units, grams, all core fields present. Real sheets like Vision's and KB's resemble the clean and moderate tiers in layout, so the clean tier is a fair proxy for the easiest real documents only.

## 4. Verdict

**MIXED.**

Reasons:
- On layout and wording the benchmark is EASIER than real sheets: one product per document, always a per-component weight in plain g/kg/oz/mg next to a unit, closed vocabularies, no tolerances, no variants or SKU tables, no recycled-content or blend wording, no non-English text, no icon-only information. These touch the core slots (weight, material, colour) that carry most of the 90% bar.
- On inconsistency and degradation it is HARDER: intra-document conflicts, buried prose, mixed units and image-only scans are rare or absent in my real sample, and they drive the two harder pass marks (80% on messy, 80% conflict flag).
- Net effect: a pass on the synthetic set (90% overall) most likely overstates accuracy on real sheets for weight, colour and material; a miss on the messy tier or the conflict bar would partly reflect over-hard synthetic conditions. The synthetic set is a fair test of "can the model follow the extraction rules and cite sources", not of "will it be right on a supplier's sheet". The pass marks are fixed and I do not suggest changing them. Evidence limits: 13 documents, English search, few UK suppliers, no real scans.

### Concrete changes (at most 5), and an optional public set

Do not alter the 30 test sheets or their seed after any run on them has been scored. Put these into a separate "realism supplement" set (new seed, about 10 sheets), run and reported separately like the public-PDF check in amendment 7, with any scorer change dated before its first run.

1. **Weight forms.** Add sheets where weight appears as "37.00gm ± 2.00gm", "1.38 oz (+/- 0.04 oz)", and as a bare number under a row label "Weight (g)"; truth is the nominal value. Fix or amend `parse_weight` and `quote_ok` first so correct answers are not scored wrong ("gm", unit in label).
2. **Weight absent but not "missing" in the usual way.** Film, label and paper components that give only gsm, µm, gauge or case weight in lb or kg; truth is `missing` for unit weight. Tests fabrication from areal weight.
3. **Multi-variant and one-item sheets.** A catalogue-style sheet with 5 to 15 SKU rows or thickness columns, with the target item code in the title and in the truth. Also single-component sheets with "various closure options", secondary-packaging lines without weights (inner liner, outer box, pallet) and no component list.
4. **Material and colour wording.** Blends ("rPET 30% + PET 70%"), recycled-content text inside the material cell, "Finish: Clear" with no Colour row, colour codes ("WhiteSolid W1229"), "Variable Coloured", plus marketing recyclability lines ("100% recyclable", "Recyclable*") and resin-code text. Decide in writing what truth says for a blend before generating.
5. **Layout.** A two-block layout where all labels are in one column block and values in another (reading-order scramble), a multi-column unit-in-header table, one page of Spanish or French, and an AES-encrypted file, to check the pipeline reads it.

**Optional small public test set (links only).** Rows 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12 above are suitable for hand labelling, plus rows 5, 6, 8 for the "weight absent" and "multi-variant" cases. None has a licence found that permits copying, so the repo would hold only the URLs, access date and my hand-written labels (not the PDFs), and a run would download them locally. Amendment 7 already says the extractor's result on public PDFs is reported separately and does not change the pass mark.
