# Part 1: Instructions to Tenderers

## 1. Background

Cinderwick Town Council (fictional) owns and operates Cinderwick Leisure Centre, a busy facility serving about 380,000 visits a year. The Council wants a specialist cleaning contractor to keep the centre safe, hygienic and welcoming, with particular attention to wet areas where hygiene and slip risks are highest.

## 2. How to respond

Tenders must be returned through the Council's procurement portal by the deadline above. Late tenders are not accepted. The tender should consist of:

1. The Compliance Declaration in Part 3.
2. Answers to the Method Questions in Part 5, in a single Word or PDF document.
3. The Price Form in Part 6.
4. Copies of insurance certificates and accreditation.

## 3. General rules on answer length

Unless stated otherwise each response is limited to 500 words. Where a question gives a different limit, that limit applies. Tables, diagrams and charts are allowed but any text within them counts towards the limit. Anything above the limit will be disregarded by evaluators.

## 4. Clarifications

Questions go through the portal. Answers will be shared with all tenderers, anonymised. No clarifications will be answered in the final 10 working days before the deadline.

## 5. Conflicts and canvassing

Tenderers must declare any conflict of interest. Canvassing members or officers will lead to disqualification.

## 6. Tender validity

Tenders remain valid for 120 days from the closing date.

## 7. Site visit

A walk-round of the centre will be offered on Wednesday 22 September 2027 at 10:00 and 14:00. Attendance is advised, not mandatory. Visits take place while the centre is open, so tenderers should wear sensible footwear. Please do not photograph members of the public.

# Part 2: Specification

## 1. The facility

| Area | Description | Approx. area (m2) |
|---|---|---|
| Pool hall | 25 m six-lane main pool, learner pool, poolside, plant-room access route | 1,350 |
| Changing rooms | Village-style changing, 2 group changing rooms, 3 accessible Changing Places style rooms, showers | 780 |
| Sports hall | 4-court hall, activity studio, storage | 1,100 |
| Fitness suite | 70-station gym, studio, stretch area | 620 |
| Reception and cafe | Public foyer, soft play seating, cafe seating (the cafe itself is cleaned by its operator) | 540 |
| Offices and staff areas | Management offices, staff room, first-aid room | 210 |
| Toilets | 6 public toilet blocks | 180 |
| External | Entrance forecourt, bin store, cycle shelter | 400 |

## 2. Opening hours

The centre opens Monday to Friday 06:30 to 22:00, Saturday 07:30 to 20:00 and Sunday 08:00 to 19:00. It is closed on Christmas Day and Boxing Day. Cleaning is therefore carried out in two ways: continuous attendant cleaning during opening hours and a night clean from 22:00 to 02:00 plus an early clean from 04:30 to 06:30 on weekdays.

## 3. Staffing minimum

The Contractor must provide the following minimum resource:

- A site supervisor on duty at all times the centre is open, with a handheld radio to the Duty Manager.
- At least 3 attendants on weekday day shifts, 4 at peak times (15:30 to 20:30), and 3 at weekends.
- A night team of at least 5 operatives, 22:00 to 02:00.
- Specialist deep-clean team for 6 planned closure days per year (2 days each for the two annual shut-downs and 1 day each in the spring and autumn).

## 4. Frequencies

| Area | Task | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Changing rooms and showers | Inspect, clean and disinfect, remove hair and litter | Hourly checks; full clean 4 times per day |
| Toilets | Clean, disinfect, restock | Every 90 minutes, recorded on checklists |
| Poolside | Remove litter, mop, disinfect handrails and benches | Hourly checks; full clean twice daily |
| Poolside floor | Machine scrub | Nightly |
| Pool hall walls, ceiling vents, grilles | Wash and descale | Quarterly |
| Gym equipment | Wipe and disinfect between peak periods | At least 4 times daily and nightly full clean |
| Sports hall floor | Dust-mop and damp-mop | Daily; machine scrub weekly (with products approved by the floor manufacturer) |
| Reception, glazing, entrance | Clean glazing, sweep, mop | Daily |
| Offices | Empty bins, vacuum, wipe | 3 times per week |
| Bins and bin store | Empty and wash | Daily; deep clean monthly |
| Tile and grout descale | Wet areas | Monthly |
| High-level dusting and light fittings | All areas | 6-monthly |

## 5. Standards

- Cleaning to a standard measured against the BICSc (British Institute of Cleaning Science) Cleaning Standards Programme or equivalent.
- Compliance with the Pool Water Treatment Advisory Group (PWTAG) guidance on hygiene in pool hall areas so far as cleaning affects it, and with the Health and Safety Executive's guidance HSG179 "Managing health and safety in swimming pools".
- COSHH assessments for every chemical used, held on site, reviewed annually.
- Legionella-aware practices: showers are flushed weekly in any area closed for more than 7 days, under the Council's own legionella plan; the Contractor must assist and record.
- Non-slip floor maintenance regime consistent with the manufacturer's guidance.
- Infection control: colour-coded equipment, clear separation of toilet and non-toilet cloths and mops.

## 6. Chemicals

Chemicals used in wet areas must be compatible with pool water treatment, in order to avoid effects on chlorine levels. The Contractor must submit a list of intended chemicals, with safety data sheets, for approval by the Duty Manager before use. Chemicals must not be decanted into unlabelled containers.

## 7. Waste

General waste, mixed recycling and clinical-type waste (sanitary, plasters, sharps) must be segregated. The Contractor will not handle sharps other than in line with the centre's sharps procedure. All waste to the centre's bin store. A waste transfer record is maintained by the Council.

## 8. Incident response

The Contractor must be able to respond within 15 minutes, at any time the centre is open, to bodily fluid incidents, including in the pool hall, and to complete a bodily fluid clean-up to a documented procedure. A deep clean kit must be on site at all times.

## 9. TUPE

The Council understands that 19 cleaning staff are employed by the current contractor, working a combined 460 hours a week. Tenderers must assume TUPE will apply. Information will be provided in the data room to shortlisted bidders at the invitation stage only. Please state in your response how you will manage this.

## 10. Safeguarding and vetting

The centre hosts children's swimming lessons, holiday clubs and youth sessions. All Contractor staff who work in the centre during opening hours must hold an enhanced DBS check (child workforce where applicable) and must complete the Council's safeguarding awareness induction before starting. Staff must not use changing rooms in a way that conflicts with the Council's changing-room policy and must be appropriately uniformed.

## 11. KPIs and monitoring

| Indicator | Target |
|---|---|
| Joint inspection scores | At least 90 percent of items pass |
| Checklists completed on time | 98 percent |
| Bodily fluid response | Within 15 minutes, 100 percent |
| Customer complaints about cleanliness | Fewer than 8 per month |
| Staff absence covered with trained cover | 100 percent |

The Contractor will attend a monthly meeting with the Leisure Manager and provide a monthly performance report.

# Part 4: Evaluation

## 1. Award criteria

The contract will be awarded to the tenderer submitting the most advantageous tender, based on the following weighting.

| Criterion | Weighting |
|---|---|
| Quality (Questions 1 to 9 in Part 5) | 70% |
| Price | 30% |

Social value is assessed within the quality score (Question 8, 8%).

## 2. Scoring

Each question is marked out of 5.

- 0: no answer or wholly unacceptable
- 1: poor, major concerns
- 2: below expectations, limited evidence
- 3: acceptable, meets the requirement
- 4: good, credible and well evidenced
- 5: excellent, exceeds the requirement with convincing evidence

The mark is multiplied by (weighting / 5) to give the weighted score. A mark of 0 on Questions 1 or 4 means rejection.

## 3. Price

The lowest price receives 30. Others are scored in proportion: 30 x (lowest price divided by tenderer price). The price is the total for the first two years.

# Part 5: Method Questions

Please answer using the numbering below. The weighting is the percentage of the total score (out of 100).

## Question 1: Cleaning method for wet areas (weighting 10%)

Explain how you will clean and disinfect the pool hall, poolside, changing rooms and showers to maintain hygiene and reduce slip risk during opening hours and at night. Include the equipment, chemicals, checking regime and how staff will be positioned at busy times.

Response limit: no more than 750 words.

## Question 2: Staffing and supervision (weighting 8%)

Set out your staffing model for the minimum resource in Part 2, section 3, including shift patterns, supervisor cover, the use of agency or bank staff, and absence cover.

## Question 3: Mobilisation (weighting 8%)

Describe your plan to take over the service from 3 January 2028, including the TUPE process, recruitment, training, equipment and a handover that avoids any gap in service during the first two weeks.

## Question 4: Infection control and incident response (weighting 10%)

Describe how you will handle bodily fluid incidents, outbreaks of infection (for example norovirus, verrucas and skin infections) and deep cleans, including response times, personal protective equipment, training and waste disposal.

Response limit: no more than 750 words.

## Question 5: Quality monitoring (weighting 8%)

Describe how you will check quality, record it, report against the KPIs in Part 2 section 11 and put right poor performance.

## Question 6: Health, safety and chemicals (weighting 8%)

Explain how you will manage health and safety, including risk assessments, COSHH, safe systems for working near water, manual handling and safeguarding of members of the public (including children) while cleaning.

## Question 7: Business continuity (weighting 6%)

Tell us how you will keep the centre operating if there is a sudden loss of staff, a plant failure leading to a partial closure, or severe weather.

## Question 8: Social value (weighting 8%)

Describe the social value you will deliver through this contract. We are particularly interested in opportunities for local residents, young people and people with disabilities to take up work and training, and in actions to reduce the carbon impact of cleaning.

Word limit: 400

## Question 9: Innovation and continuous improvement (weighting 4%)

Describe two improvements you would introduce in the first 12 months (for example technology, equipment or processes), with the expected benefit and how it will be measured.

Total quality weighting: 70%.

# Part 6: Price Form

## 1. What to price

Tenderers must provide the following, in pounds sterling and excluding VAT, in the Price Form spreadsheet.

1. A fixed monthly price for the service in Part 2, covering all labour, supervision, equipment, chemicals and overheads. Consumables for the public are bought by the Council.
2. Hourly rates for additional cleaning (weekday, evening, weekend and bank holiday).
3. A lump sum per planned deep-clean closure day (6 per year).
4. A rate for an emergency response outside the specified resource.
5. Mobilisation costs shown as a one-off sum.

## 2. Price evaluation

The evaluated price is 24 months of the fixed monthly price plus 12 deep clean closure days plus mobilisation, plus 200 hours at the additional weekday hourly rate and 100 hours at the weekend rate. Prices for the first year are fixed. In later years, any increase is limited to the percentage rise in the National Living Wage, evidenced in the open-book wage schedule.

## 3. Payment and invoicing

The Council pays within 30 days of a valid invoice. Invoices are monthly in arrears with a performance report attached.

## 4. Abnormally low prices

Tenders where price appears abnormally low will be queried and may be excluded if the tenderer cannot show that statutory pay, holiday and on-costs are covered.
