## 3. Specification

### 3.1 Concierge service

Concierge staff are to be provided at three high-rise residential blocks.

| Block | Location | Hours of concierge | Number of flats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alder Court | Riverside estate | 24 hours, 365 days, one concierge at all times | 112 |
| Beech Tower | Northgate estate | 07:00 to 23:00 daily, one concierge | 96 |
| Cedar Point | Hillcrest estate | 16:00 to 08:00 daily, one concierge, two on Friday and Saturday nights | 84 |

Duties of concierge officers include controlling access, checking contractors and visitors against the Association's approved list, monitoring CCTV in the lobby, dealing with resident enquiries, reporting repairs, carrying out hourly internal checks of communal areas, and checking fire safety equipment daily. Concierge officers must support evacuation procedures in line with the Association's fire strategy, including maintaining the Personal Emergency Evacuation Plan list and assisting the fire service on arrival. They must not enter a resident's home except in an emergency and with a second person present.

### 3.2 Estate patrol

A two-person mobile patrol will cover the Association's 14 estates, 7 days a week from 18:00 to 02:00, rising to 22:00 to 04:00 on the nights of 31 October to 6 November. Patrols will respond to reports of anti-social behaviour, check lockable bin stores, bike stores and garage courts, deter drug dealing, and escort staff on welfare visits when requested.

Patrol priorities will be set weekly by the Neighbourhood Safety Team using a hotspot list. The standard patrol pattern requires each of the 14 estates to be visited at least once per shift, and the top five hotspots at least three times. Patrol officers must not intervene physically except to protect life or in self-defence, and must record all contact using body-worn video, in line with the Association's data protection policy.

Response times for the patrol to reach a reported incident within the patrol area: 20 minutes for an urgent matter (violence, a threat to life, fire, drug dealing in progress) and 60 minutes for a non-urgent matter such as noise or a suspicious vehicle.

### 3.3 Void property security

The Association has about 380 voids in a year, typically empty for 21 to 35 days. The contractor will, on a task order from the voids team, fit or remove temporary steel security screens or alarmed door sets, carry out a weekly external check of each void, and respond to alerts from void alarm units. Any break-in must be reported to the voids team within one hour and to the police on the same day.

Void response targets: screens fitted within 24 hours of a task order; weekly check completed for 100% of voids on the schedule; an alarm response within 60 minutes. The Association anticipates around 380 screen fit and remove visits a year. This service is subject to the ability to deploy a trade-qualified operative, with a van.

### 3.4 Assignment instructions and reporting

For every block and for the patrol, the contractor will write assignment instructions in a form agreed with the Association. Serious incidents must be reported by phone to the Association's duty manager within 30 minutes and in writing within 24 hours. A weekly summary report will be submitted every Monday, covering incidents, response times, patrol coverage and void activity. A monthly performance report will be provided ahead of each monthly review meeting.

### 3.5 TUPE

The current concierge contractor employs 21 staff on the contract. The Association believes TUPE will apply to concierge staff but not to patrol or void security as these services are new in this form. Anonymised information is in Appendix 2. The Association gives no warranty.

### 3.6 Living Wage

All staff working on the contract must be paid at least the real Living Wage, increased within six months of announcement.

## Appendix 4: Escalation Protocol Summary

Concierge and patrol officers must follow this order of escalation. For any threat to life or an offence in progress, call 999 first and then the Association's duty manager. For a fire, raise the alarm, call the fire and rescue service and follow the building evacuation plan. For a safeguarding concern about a child or adult at risk, report the same day to the Association's safeguarding lead, using the referral form, and to the police or local authority where there is immediate danger. For a repair that affects safety, such as a failed lift, a broken door entry system or a communal fire door, report to the repairs desk and record the action in the log. For noise, litter or other low-level anti-social behaviour, record the details and pass to the Neighbourhood Safety Team the next working day.

Officers must make a written record at the time or as soon as it is safe to do so. Records must be factual, dated and timed, and must not include opinion about individuals.

## Appendix 5: Resident Engagement

The Association expects the contractor to take part in quarterly resident forums at each block, to respond courteously to residents' complaints, and to accept feedback from the Residents' Panel. Concierge officers should know the residents who have additional needs, within the limits of the data sharing agreement, and should be a visible and reassuring presence. Officers who are found to have behaved rudely or inappropriately towards a resident will be removed from the contract on request.
