## Section 3: Specification of Services

### 3.1 Background

Brackenmere District Council (the Council) is a district authority serving around 96,000 residents. It operates a civic centre, a leisure centre, two depots, a market hall, public parks, car parks and a town centre CCTV scheme. Security is currently provided under several short contracts which expire on 31 August 2027. The Council wishes to consolidate them into one integrated contract with a single point of accountability.

### 3.2 Overview of services

The contract has four service elements:

- Static manned guarding and reception security.
- Mobile patrols of parks, car parks and vacant premises.
- Keyholding and alarm response for Council premises.
- CCTV monitoring from a control room.

### 3.3 Sites and posts

| Ref | Site | Service | Hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Civic Centre, Market Square | One static officer (reception and building security) | 07:00 to 19:00 Monday to Friday; 08:00 to 16:00 on council meeting evenings extended to 22:00 (about 18 evenings a year) |
| A2 | Waterside Leisure Centre | One static officer (night security, customer-facing at close) | 21:00 to 06:00, 7 nights a week |
| A3 | Millbrook Depot | One static officer at vehicle gate | 18:00 to 06:00 Monday to Friday; 24 hours at weekends |
| B1 | Parks patrol (6 parks) | Mobile patrol, one vehicle, one officer | Dusk to 02:00, 7 days; additional daytime patrols in school holidays |
| B2 | Car parks patrol (3 multi-storey, 4 surface) | Mobile patrol, one vehicle, one officer | 18:00 to 02:00, 7 days |
| C1 | 24 Council premises | Keyholding and alarm response | 24 hours, 365 days |
| D1 | Town centre CCTV | Monitoring of 32 cameras, one operator, second operator on Friday and Saturday nights | 24 hours, 365 days |

The Council estimates 31,500 static guarding hours, 9,800 mobile patrol hours and 1,150 CCTV operator hours per annum above the baseline staffing. These volumes are indicative, not guaranteed. The Council may increase or reduce hours by up to 20% without a change of rates.

### 3.4 Patrol routes

Mobile patrols must visit each park at least twice per shift, at irregular intervals, with at least one dwell of 15 minutes at the pavilion or main gate. Car parks must be visited at least three times per shift, including walking each level of the three multi-storey sites. Patrol routes will be agreed in the first month and reviewed quarterly using incident data. Patrol officers must record visits using a digital guard-tour system with time-stamped checkpoints.

### 3.5 Keyholding and alarm response

The Contractor must provide a staffed 24-hour alarm receiving and keyholder response service. Response times from the point of activation notification are:

| Priority | Description | Maximum response time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirmed intruder or fire alarm at a high-risk site (Civic Centre, Depot stores, Leisure Centre plant) | 30 minutes |
| 2 | Unconfirmed intruder alarm at any other site | 45 minutes |
| 3 | Technical alarm or non-urgent fault | 90 minutes |

Keyholders must not enter a premises alone where there are signs of forced entry. The police must be called first, and the keyholder must wait at a safe distance. Keys must be stored in line with BS 7984 and audited monthly. Annual keyholder numbers are expected to be around 600 alarm activations.

### 3.6 CCTV monitoring

The control room must operate in line with BS 7958 and the Surveillance Camera Commissioner's code of practice. Operators must hold an SIA Public Space Surveillance (CCTV) licence. The Contractor must provide a secure link to the Council's camera network and a dedicated police radio link. Operators must log incidents, support evidence requests under the Council's data sharing agreement, and report camera faults within one hour.

### 3.7 Assignment instructions

The Contractor must produce and maintain site-specific assignment instructions for every post, patrol and keyholding site, approved by the Council's Facilities Manager before service commencement. These must cover access, emergency procedures, lone working arrangements, key control, escalation contacts and local risks. Instructions must be reviewed at least every six months and after any serious incident.

### 3.8 Incident reporting and escalation

All incidents must be notified to the Council's duty officer by telephone within 30 minutes of a serious incident (assault, fire, break-in, safeguarding concern, or major disruption). A written incident report must reach the Contract Manager within 24 hours. Routine incidents must be recorded in the electronic daily occurrence log. Monthly reports must summarise incidents, response-time performance, patrol completion and complaints.

### 3.9 Key performance indicators

| KPI | Target |
|---|---|
| Post fill rate | 99.5% of contracted hours |
| Patrol checkpoint completion | 98% |
| Keyholder response within time | 95% |
| Incident report submitted within 24 hours | 100% |
| Complaints acknowledged within 2 working days | 100% |
| Operative turnover per annum | Below 25% |

Service credits of up to 5% of monthly charges may apply where targets are not met for two consecutive months.

### 3.10 TUPE

The Council believes that the transfer of staff from the current providers may constitute a relevant transfer under the Transfer of Undertakings (Protection of Employment) Regulations 2006. Anonymised employee liability information for 34 staff is included in Appendix C. The Council gives no warranty as to its accuracy. Bidders must price on the basis that TUPE applies and must state any assumptions made. Bidders must not make contact with incumbent staff or providers.

### 3.11 Living Wage and employment standards

The Contractor must pay all operatives working on the contract at least the real Living Wage as set by the Living Wage Foundation, increasing within six months of each annual announcement. Zero-hours contracts must not be used unless requested by the employee.

### 3.12 Uniform, vehicles and equipment

All officers must wear a clearly identified uniform with SIA licence displayed. Patrol vehicles must be low-emission, with a minimum of Euro 6 diesel or hybrid. The Council expects a plan to move to electric patrol vehicles by the end of year three.

## Section 5: Evaluation Method

### 5.1 Award criteria

The contract will be awarded to the most advantageous tender, assessed on the following basis.

| Criterion | Weighting |
|---|---|
| Quality (Section 6) | 60% |
| Price (Section 7) | 40% |
| Total | 100% |

### 5.2 Scoring of quality questions

Each quality question is scored from 0 to 5 by a moderated panel. The score is multiplied by the question's weighting divided by 5 to give a weighted score out of the percentage shown.

| Score | Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Unacceptable | No response, or response fails to address the requirement |
| 1 | Poor | Major weaknesses; low confidence in delivery |
| 2 | Weak | Some relevant detail but significant gaps or lack of evidence |
| 3 | Satisfactory | Meets the requirement with adequate detail |
| 4 | Good | Meets the requirement with good detail and relevant evidence |
| 5 | Excellent | Exceeds the requirement with convincing, specific evidence |

### 5.3 Minimum quality threshold

Any bidder scoring 2 or below on any question marked with an asterisk, or an overall quality score below 50% of the available quality marks, may be excluded. The questions marked with an asterisk are Q2, Q5 and Q12.

### 5.4 Price evaluation

The lowest compliant total price will receive the full 40%. Other bids will receive a score calculated as: (lowest price divided by bid price) multiplied by 40. Abnormally low tenders will be investigated and may be rejected under the Procurement Act 2023.

### 5.5 Final score

Quality and price scores will be added. In a tie, the bidder with the higher quality score will be ranked first.

## Section A: Delivery Model (max 500 words per question)

### Q1 Staffing and rostering (5%)

Describe how you will resource and roster the static posts, mobile patrols, keyholding and CCTV control room, including how you will cover sickness, annual leave, and peaks such as council meeting evenings, school holidays and Christmas lights events. Explain how you will keep to a 99.5% fill rate and manage working time rules and fatigue.

### Q2 Recruitment and vetting (5%)*

Set out your approach to recruiting, screening and onboarding staff in line with BS 7858, including how you will verify SIA licences, right to work and references, and what you do where a candidate has gaps in their employment history. Say how long vetting takes and how you will ensure no one works unscreened on this contract.

### Q3 Training and development (4%)

Describe the induction and ongoing training you will provide, including conflict management, first aid at work, fire marshal duties, equality and disability awareness, and site-specific training. Explain how you will record and refresh training and how it will be adapted for CCTV operators.

### Q4 Supervision and welfare of lone workers (4%)

Many of the posts and all patrols are staffed by lone workers at night. Explain how you will supervise them, how you will check on their welfare, what technology you will use (for example, lone worker devices), and how you will respond if an officer does not check in.

### Q5 Incident response and escalation (4%)*

Explain how you will respond to and escalate the following scenarios: (a) a confirmed intruder at the Millbrook Depot at 03:00; (b) a group of young people causing disturbance in a park, some apparently vulnerable; (c) a keyholder arriving at a site where the alarm has sounded and a door has been forced. Describe the decision-making, the use of the police and the Council's duty officer, and the reporting that follows.

### Q6 Use of technology (3%)

Describe the systems you will use for guard tour, incident reporting, rostering and client access to data. Explain how the Council will see real-time information and how your systems handle data protection and cyber security.

## Section B: Mobilisation and Performance (no more than 350 words per response)

### Q7 Mobilisation and transition (5%)

Provide a mobilisation plan for the period 21 June to 31 August 2027. Cover TUPE consultation, recruitment, vetting, assignment instructions, key and equipment handover, the CCTV link, and the risks to a smooth start and how you will manage them. Include a table or timeline if it helps (diagrams count toward the limit).

### Q8 KPIs and reporting (3%)

Explain how you will measure and report against the KPIs in 3.9, how you will use data to improve performance, and what you will do when a target is missed. Give an example of a monthly report format.

### Q9 Complaints (2%)

Describe your complaints procedure for complaints from the public, Council staff and employees, including timescales and how learning is shared.

### Q10 Contract management (3%)

Explain how you will manage the contract, including named roles, review meetings, escalation, change control for variations in hours, and how you will maintain continuity if your account manager leaves.

### Q11 Insurance and accreditation confirmation (Pass/Fail)

Confirm that the accreditations and insurances stated in Section 4 will remain valid throughout the term, and describe what you will do if any lapses. This question is pass/fail and is not scored. A response which does not confirm continuity will be rejected. Word limit: 150.

## Section C: Compliance, Resilience and Value

### Q12 Health and safety (3%)*

Describe your health and safety management system for this contract, including risk assessment of patrol routes and lone working, accident reporting, and the safe use of vehicles. Give your RIDDOR record for the last three years.

### Q13 Environmental sustainability (2%) (max 300 words)

Explain how you will reduce the environmental impact of the service, including patrol vehicles, energy use in the control room and use of paper. Set out your plan for a transition to electric vehicles.

### Q14 Business continuity (3%) (max 400 words)

Describe your business continuity plan for events such as severe weather, fuel shortages, a failure of the control room, a cyber attack or a pandemic. Explain how you will maintain the 24-hour keyholding response and CCTV monitoring if the control room becomes unavailable.

### Q15 Safeguarding and public interaction (3%)

This question has two parts and a combined limit of 500 words.

- 15(a) Explain how your staff are trained to recognise and report safeguarding concerns involving children and adults at risk, including rough sleepers and people in mental health crisis.
- 15(b) Describe how you will ensure officers deal with the public fairly and proportionately, including your approach to use of force, body-worn video and complaints from members of the public.

### Q16 Case study (4%) (maximum 2 sides of A4)

Provide one case study of a comparable contract you delivered in the last three years, including a combined keyholding and patrol element in a local authority or public-sector setting. Describe the challenge, what you did, measurable outcomes, and lessons learned. Include the client's name only if the client has agreed to be a reference.

### Q17 Social value (7%) (max 600 words)

The Council's social value priorities are: local employment and skills; support for the local economy; fair work; and reducing carbon emissions. Describe the commitments you will make in each area, with measurable targets, how you will deliver them and how you will report progress. Commitments will form part of the contract. Examples might include the number of local residents recruited, apprenticeships, work placements for people furloughed from long-term unemployment, spend with local suppliers, and hours of volunteering.

## Appendix F: Performance Management Regime

The Contract Manager will hold a monthly performance meeting with the Contractor's account manager. A formal quarterly review will examine KPI trends, incident analysis, patrol route effectiveness, training records, social value delivery and any proposed improvements. The Council may carry out unannounced audits of any post or patrol at any time, and the Contractor must give access to its systems and records on request. Where performance falls below target for two consecutive months, the Contractor must submit a written remedial plan within five working days. Persistent failure may lead to step-in rights, increased service credits, or termination for material breach.

The Contractor must make an officer available to attend, at no extra cost, up to six community safety or partnership meetings a year, including the town centre safety partnership and the parks user groups. The Contractor must also supply de-identified data to the Council's community safety team for analysis, in a format agreed at mobilisation.
