## 3. Specification

### 3.1 About the Trust

Halden Vale Community Assets Trust (fictional) owns and operates three leisure centres, a covered market, a business centre, four community halls and a small industrial estate on behalf of its parent council. The Trust wants one provider for remote monitoring and attendance, so that its sites are protected when closed.

### 3.2 Service A: CCTV and intruder alarm monitoring

The provider will operate a 24 hour, 365 day alarm receiving and video monitoring centre which meets BS 5979 Category II (or equivalent EN 50518 standards for alarm receiving) and BS 7958 for CCTV. The Trust has 11 sites with 138 cameras, of which 64 are fitted with video analytics for intrusion detection, and 19 sites with intruder alarm panels. The provider will:

- receive and verify alarm activations and analytics events;
- use live audio or video challenge where fitted;
- dispatch the keyholder or police according to site protocols;
- keep audit logs for 31 days (video evidence for 31 days unless exported);
- raise camera and system faults with the Trust's maintainer within 1 hour.

Verified activations must be acted on within 90 seconds of receipt for priority 1 sites (the market and the three leisure centres).

### 3.3 Service B: Keyholding and alarm response

The provider will hold keys for all 19 sites and attend activations. A mobile officer must be on site within:

| Site category | Sites | Maximum response time |
|---|---|---|
| Category 1: leisure centres and covered market | 4 | 25 minutes |
| Category 2: business centre and industrial estate units | 6 | 40 minutes |
| Category 3: community halls and other sites | 9 | 60 minutes |

An average of 550 call-outs a year is expected (around 45% false alarms). The provider must carry out a full internal and external check on arrival, secure the premises, wait for the repairer if required, and report to the Trust by 08:00 the following morning. Where the police recommend that nobody enters, the keyholder must remain outside and wait. The provider must audit keys at least quarterly to BS 7984-1, and key codes must never be written on key tags.

### 3.4 Service C: Opening and closing

For the 4 community halls and the market the provider will unlock at 06:30 and lock at the time stated in the booking schedule (usually between 22:00 and 23:30), with 100% punctuality and a handwritten or digital sign-off. Around 4,400 visits are forecast per year.

### 3.5 Service D: Scheduled mobile patrols

The industrial estate and business centre will be patrolled twice nightly at irregular times, with a drive-through and an external check on all units, using a checkpoint system. About 730 patrol visits per year are expected.

### 3.6 Reporting

An immediate electronic alert must be sent to the Trust's duty officer for incidents classed as serious (break-in, fire, flood, assault on staff, or safeguarding concern). A full written report is required by 12:00 on the next working day. A monthly report will show all activations and their causes, response times, patrol completion, false alarm analysis and recommendations for reducing false alarms. Quarterly reviews will examine trends and agree actions.

### 3.7 Assignment instructions

For each site the provider must prepare a site folder or digital record covering contacts, alarm zones, access, hazards, escalation, opening and closing times and special instructions. The Trust's Property Manager will approve them before service start.

### 3.8 TUPE

The Trust does not believe that TUPE applies. The current services are delivered by two providers from regional control rooms and the keyholding is part of a wider national contract. Bidders may take their own advice and must state any assumption in their pricing.

### 3.9 Data protection

The provider will act as a data processor for CCTV footage and alarm data. It must sign the Trust's data processing agreement, apply the Surveillance Camera Code, store footage only in the UK, and respond to subject access or police requests within the timescales the Trust sets.

Evaluators may award intermediate scores such as 40 or 60 where justified.

## Appendix 3: Response Protocols Summary

For an intruder alarm, the monitoring centre will first check the signal type and zone, then use live video or audio challenge where fitted. If an intruder is confirmed, the centre calls the police, using the unique reference number held by the Trust, and dispatches the keyholder. The keyholder does not enter until the police advise that it is safe, or arrive at the same time. For a fire alarm, the centre calls the fire and rescue service immediately and then informs the Trust's duty officer. For a flood or other technical alarm, the keyholder attends, isolates the supply where it is safe to do so and informs the duty officer. For a confirmed break-in, the keyholder secures the premises temporarily, arranges boarding up through the Trust's repairer and stays with the premises until it is secure.

All actions are time-stamped and stored in the audit log. Each activation must be closed with a cause code, so that the false alarm analysis in the monthly report is accurate. The Trust may audit a sample of calls and recordings at any time on 24 hours' notice.

## Appendix 4: Opening and Closing Standards

Officers carrying out opening and closing must check that the premises are secure on arrival, walk through all public areas, switch on lights and heating where instructed, and report any damage or hazard before the first users enter. At closing, officers must check that all users have left, that fire doors are closed, that windows and external doors are locked, that the alarm is set and that the area is free of any hazard. A digital record with a photograph of the alarm panel's set status must be uploaded for each closing visit.
