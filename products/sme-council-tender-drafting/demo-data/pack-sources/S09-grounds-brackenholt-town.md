# Section A: Instructions to Bidders

## 1. About the Council

Brackenholt Town Council (fictional) is a first-tier local council serving a market town of approximately 14,000 residents. It owns and maintains a portfolio of parks, recreation grounds, a cemetery, a closed churchyard, verges, shrub beds and four equipped play areas. The Council wishes to appoint a single contractor to deliver grounds maintenance on a measured-frequency basis, with a separate schedule of provisional sums for ad hoc works.

# Section B: Specification of Services

## 1. Overview

The contractor will maintain all sites listed in the site schedule to the standards in this section from the contract start date. Frequencies are the minimum number of visits per year. The contractor must adjust the timing of visits to growth and weather so that the standards below are met at all times.

## 2. Site schedule

| Site | Area | Grass cuts per year | Hedges and shrub beds | Seasonal tasks | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Millbank Recreation Ground | 6.2 ha | 18 (amenity) plus 2 meadow cuts | 420 m hedge, 0.12 ha shrub beds | Line-marking of two junior pitches, leaf clearance Oct to Dec | Includes pavilion surrounds and a skatepark |
| Castle Street Gardens | 1.4 ha | 22 (ornamental, fine finish) | 0.35 ha formal beds, 90 m hedge | Spring and summer bedding (supply and plant 2,500 plants), weekly bin emptying | Heavily used; high standard |

## 3. Standards of work

- Amenity grass must be maintained between 25 mm and 60 mm in height at all times and cut to 35 mm to 40 mm on ornamental lawns.
- Arisings from amenity grass up to 60 mm may be mulched where weather permits. Arisings from longer grass and meadow areas must be collected and removed from site within 48 hours.
- Hedges must be cut twice a year outside the bird nesting season (not between 1 March and 31 August unless a nesting check is recorded and cutting is agreed with the Council). Cut hedges to the profile specified in the site schedule.
- Shrub beds must be at least 95% free of weeds at every inspection, edged and mulched once a year with peat-free mulch.
- Litter and dog waste must be cleared before every grass cut and at each visit to a play area. Litter must not be left in hedges or beds.
- All grass edges and paths must be trimmed at every cut.
- All machinery must be used safely, with debris guards and with grass and clippings removed from hard surfaces before leaving the area.

## 4. Play area inspections

- Weekly visual inspections of the four play areas, recorded on the contractor's app or paper log.
- A quarterly operational inspection and an annual inspection by a qualified inspector, following BS EN 1176 and BS EN 1177, reported within 5 working days.
- Any hazard presenting a risk of serious injury must be made safe or taped off within 2 hours and reported to the Town Clerk by phone.
- Minor repairs up to £150 each may be carried out without prior approval.

## 5. Cemetery and churchyard

- All work in the cemetery must be done with sensitivity. No machinery to be used within 30 metres of a burial ceremony. Staff must stop work and keep clear of mourners.
- Grass between graves must be cut or strimmed so that no memorial is damaged. Any damage must be reported the same day.
- Grave preparation and backfilling is outside this contract, but the contractor must make the site tidy within 24 hours of notice.
- The closed churchyard is subject to a diocese faculty condition. No chemical treatment of any kind may be used.

## 6. Herbicide and chemical use

- Herbicide use is limited to glyphosate-based or approved alternatives, applied only by PA1/PA6 qualified staff to hard surfaces, tree bases, bed edges and kerb lines, and not more than twice a year per site.
- No herbicide in the churchyard, the wood, the meadow, or within 5 metres of a watercourse or play equipment.
- The contractor must record each application, with product, area, weather and operator, and send the record to the Council within 7 days.
- The Council actively encourages bidders to propose non-chemical methods such as hot foam, mechanical brushing or electric weeders.

## 7. Winter gritting of Council sites

- Gritting of Council car parks, the pavilion access and the Castle Street Gardens paths during frost or snow forecasts between 1 November and 31 March.
- Pre-treat before 07:00 on forecast days. Maximum response time on notice is 3 hours, 7 days a week.
- The contractor supplies its own salt and spreader. Up to 25 call-outs a winter are assumed, priced in the schedule.

## 8. Biodiversity areas

- St Aldwyn's Closed Churchyard, the Community Wood and Meadow, and 1.1 ha of verge at Highfield are designated wildlife areas. These must follow the Council's biodiversity plan: cut and collect once a year after 15 August, no fertiliser, and retain refuges and log piles.
- The contractor must not remove or damage habitat features without the Council's written consent.

## 9. Waste and arisings

- Green waste must be taken to a permitted site and weighed. Tonnage reports must be provided quarterly.
- Waste must be handled under duty-of-care rules with transfer notes kept for 2 years.
- Fly-tipping found on Council land must be reported the same day and removed up to one cubic metre without extra cost.

## 10. Reporting and inspection

- The contractor must provide a monthly report of work done, issues and any incidents.
- The Council will inspect at least monthly. Failures to meet the standards must be corrected within 5 working days or 24 hours where a safety risk.
- Key performance indicators are set out in Appendix 1.

# Section C: Contract Conditions and Council Policies

## 1. Payment and performance

The Council will pay valid monthly invoices within 30 days. Invoices must be accompanied by the monthly report and a log of work done at each site. The Council may withhold payment for any item of work not completed to standard, and deduct service credits of up to 5% of the monthly invoice where KPIs in Appendix 1 are missed in two consecutive months. The Council reserves the right to have work carried out by others where defects are not rectified in time and to recover the cost from the contractor.

## 2. Health, safety and the public

The contractor is responsible for the safety of its own staff and members of the public. It must produce a site-specific risk assessment and method statement for each site before work starts, and review it annually. Operatives must wear high-visibility clothing, eye, ear and foot protection and, where needed, face visors and chainsaw trousers. Lone working must be covered by a documented procedure, including a check-in system and a means of calling help. The contractor must report any accident or near miss that involves a member of the public to the Town Clerk within 24 hours. The contractor must comply with the Control of Pesticides Regulations and the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974.

## 3. Safeguarding

Several sites are used by children, including the Wheatsheaf Play Area, which adjoins an infant school. Operatives working at these sites during school hours or at weekends must hold a current enhanced DBS check. Operatives must not engage in conversation with children, must not photograph them, and must report any concern to the contractor's designated safeguarding lead and to the Town Clerk. Noisy machinery must not be used within 25 metres of the school boundary between 08:30 and 09:15 or between 15:00 and 15:30 on school days.

## 4. Environment

The Council declared a climate and nature emergency in 2021. The contractor must support the Council's aim to cut carbon from contracted services by 30% by 2031. This includes using battery-powered equipment where reasonably practicable, turning engines off when not in use, using only peat-free compost, reducing the use of herbicide, recycling green waste and using locally sourced materials. The contractor must report fuel use and mileage each year.

## 5. Data protection

The contractor may see personal data in the cemetery, such as grave inscriptions and burial notices, and may receive complaints from residents. It must treat all such information as confidential, use it only for the contract and report any breach to the Council within 24 hours. The Council is the controller and the contractor a processor, and a data processing agreement forms part of the contract.

## 6. Subcontracting and key personnel

The contractor must not subcontract any part of the services without the Council's written consent. Any bidder who proposes to use a subcontractor for more than 10% of the work must identify it in Section D. The contractor must name a contract manager and a deputy and must not replace them without consulting the Council.

## 7. Business continuity and exit

The contractor must maintain a business continuity plan and provide a copy to the Council on request. At the end of the contract it must co-operate with the Council and any new contractor, including supplying staff information within 6 months of the contract end for TUPE purposes, handing over records, keys and any Council property, and leaving all sites in good order.
