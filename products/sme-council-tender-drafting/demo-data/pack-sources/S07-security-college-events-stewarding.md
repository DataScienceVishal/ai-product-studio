## Part 3: Specification

### 3.1 The College

Thornbeck College (fictional) teaches about 6,500 students, of whom around 1,100 are aged 16 to 18 and 400 are adults with learning difficulties or disabilities. It operates from a main campus with a theatre, sports hall, library and a student union building, and from a smaller construction skills centre three miles away. The College holds events that bring thousands of visitors on to site.

### 3.2 Services required

The College needs three services.

1. Event security and stewarding for open days, enrolment weeks, graduation ceremonies, theatre performances, sports fixtures and student union events.
2. Campus night security for the main campus.
3. Ad hoc security for emergencies, protests, bomb threats or other unplanned requirements.

### 3.3 Typical event calendar

| Event | Approximate dates | Attendance | Cover required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spring open day | One Saturday in March | 2,500 | 18 stewards, 2 supervisors, 4 car park marshals, 8 hours |
| Summer open evening | One weekday in June | 1,800 | 12 stewards, 1 supervisor, 4 car park marshals, 5 hours |
| Enrolment weeks | 5 days in late August | 4,000 over the week | 6 stewards per day, 1 supervisor |
| Graduation ceremonies | Two days in November | 1,400 per day | 20 stewards, 2 supervisors, 6 marshals, 10 hours |
| Theatre season | About 30 evenings a year | 150 to 350 | 4 stewards, one supervisor |
| Students' Union events | About 25 evenings a year | Up to 800 | 8 door staff with SIA door supervisor licences, 1 supervisor |
| Sports fixtures | About 20 afternoons a year | Up to 300 | 3 stewards |

A minimum of 6 days' notice will be given for events where possible; the College will provide a rolling 3-month calendar. Last-minute requests with less than 48 hours' notice are expected on no more than ten occasions a year.

### 3.4 Campus night security

One licensed security officer is to be on the main campus from 19:00 to 07:00 every night of the year, with an additional officer from Thursday to Saturday during term time. Duties are to patrol buildings and grounds, secure doors and windows, respond to intruder, fire and lift alarms, supervise contractors working after hours, and support the small number of students living in the on-site residences. Patrol rounds are to be made every 90 minutes, recorded on a tour system.

### 3.5 Roles and licensing

Stewards at events not involving licensable activity do not require a licence, but the College requires that all event staff have completed a recognised steward training course (such as the Level 2 Award for Event Stewards or equivalent). Door supervisors, security officers and CCTV operators must hold the appropriate SIA licence. Supervisors must hold an SIA licence and a first aid at work certificate.

### 3.6 Safeguarding

The College is responsible for under-18 students and adults at risk. All staff working on College premises must hold a current enhanced DBS check. They must complete the College's safeguarding induction before their first shift. Staff must immediately report any concern to the Designated Safeguarding Lead using the College's referral process.

### 3.7 Counter-terrorism

Staff working at events must be trained in the principles of the Terrorism (Protection of Premises) approach, including awareness training such as ACT Awareness, and be familiar with the Run, Hide, Tell guidance, evacuation, invacuation and lockdown. They must follow the College's event risk assessment and the instructions of the Event Safety Officer.

### 3.8 Incident reporting

Any incident involving injury, assault, a safeguarding concern, a weapon or suspected weapon, a significant breach of security, or a complaint from a member of the public must be reported verbally to the Facilities Manager (or the Duty Manager out of hours) immediately and in writing within 24 hours. A post-event debrief report is required within three working days, giving numbers, incidents, lessons learned and recommendations.

### 3.9 TUPE

The College currently buys night security from a provider which employs 6 staff on the contract. Event stewarding is delivered by an agency on a call-off basis and TUPE is not expected to apply to it. Anonymised information on the 6 night officers is in Appendix B. The College gives no warranty about it.

### 3.10 Living Wage

The contractor must pay all staff the real Living Wage as a minimum.

## Part 5: Evaluation

### 5.1 Weightings

| Criterion | Weighting |
|---|---|
| Quality | 70% |
| Price | 30% |

### 5.2 Scoring scale

Quality answers are scored from 0 to 10. Scores are multiplied by the question's weighting divided by 10.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | No response or fails to meet the requirement |
| 1 to 3 | Response contains major weaknesses or is poorly evidenced |
| 4 to 6 | Response meets the requirement but lacks detail or evidence in places |
| 7 to 8 | Response is detailed and evidenced and gives confidence in delivery |
| 9 to 10 | Response is exceptional, with specific evidence and added value |

### 5.3 Price scoring

The lowest evaluated price receives 30%. Others receive a proportionate score: 30% multiplied by the lowest price divided by the bidder's price.

### 5.4 Moderation and interviews

Evaluators will score independently, then meet to agree consensus scores. The College may interview the highest-scoring bidders and may adjust scores after interview, but only for the questions the interview covers.

## Part 6: Quality Questions

There are ten questions. Total weighting is 70%.

### Question 1: Event planning and deployment (9%)

Maximum 750 words.

Explain how you will plan and resource events of different sizes, from a 30-person sports fixture to a 2,500-visitor open day. Describe how you will liaise with the College events team, work from an event risk assessment, brief staff, position stewards and manage crowd flow, queues and car parking.

### Question 2: Recruitment, vetting and the stewarding workforce (8%)

Limit: 500 words.

Describe how you will recruit and vet event stewards, supervisors and night officers, including BS 7858 screening and DBS checks, and how you will build a reliable pool of staff who are familiar with the College. Explain how you will ensure staff reliability at short notice, including your no-show rate.

### Question 3: Training (8%)

(max 500 words)

Describe the training for stewards, supervisors and night officers, including steward qualifications, conflict management, first aid, ACT Awareness, fire marshal duties, equality, and Prevent. State how you record training and how you check competence in the field.

### Question 4: Safeguarding (7%)

Maximum one side of A4.

Explain how you will protect under-18 students and adults at risk during events and night security patrols. Include how staff will report concerns, how you will manage contact with young people, and how you will handle an allegation made against one of your staff.

### Question 5: Incident response and escalation (7%)

No more than 600 words.

Explain how you will respond to and escalate the following scenarios: (a) a student collapses during a graduation ceremony; (b) a large group of non-students tries to enter the Students' Union event without tickets; (c) a bomb threat is received during a theatre performance. Describe who decides, who is informed and what happens afterwards.

### Question 6: Supervision, welfare and lone workers (7%)

Word limit: 400.

Explain how you will supervise staff at events and on campus at night, including lone working by the night officer, welfare checks, breaks and fatigue management, and how you will support staff who have faced abuse.

### Question 7: Use of technology (6%)

(max 300 words)

Describe the technology you will use for scheduling, communication, patrol tours, incident reporting, headcount, and gathering feedback. Explain how the College will access real-time information.

### Question 8: Mobilisation and transition (6%)

Maximum 1 side of A4.

Describe your plan for taking over night security from 1 August 2027, including TUPE, and for preparing for the enrolment weeks in late August, which begin within four weeks of the contract start.

### Question 9: Reporting, KPIs and contract management (6%)

Limit: 400 words.

Propose the key performance indicators you will report against, how you will report them, and how you will manage the contract, including review meetings, post-event debriefs, complaints and continuous improvement.

### Question 10: Social value and sustainability (6%)

Maximum 500 words.

The College is committed to the progression of its students. Describe the social value you will deliver, such as paid work experience and employment for students and graduates (including those with learning difficulties), local recruitment, and apprenticeships. Describe also how you will reduce the environmental impact of the service. Commitments will be monitored through the contract.
