# Section 1: Instructions to Bidders

## 1.1 Introduction

Brackenhall District Council (fictional), referred to as "the Council", invites tenders for the cleaning of its civic buildings, depots and public conveniences. The Council serves a mixed rural and market-town district and has a strong commitment to local employment, fair pay and reducing the environmental impact of its estate.

This document and its appendices together form the Invitation to Tender (ITT). Bidders must read all of it. Submitting a tender is taken as acceptance of the instructions below.

## 1.3 Submission

Tenders must be submitted through the Council's e-tendering portal before the deadline stated on the cover page. Tenders sent by email, post or hand will not be considered. Late tenders will be rejected unless the portal was demonstrably unavailable because of a fault on the Council's side.

Bidders should allow time to upload documents. The Council recommends uploading at least 24 hours before the deadline. Bidders must upload the following:

1. The completed Selection Questionnaire (Section 3).
2. Responses to every question in Section 5 in a single document, using the question numbers given.
3. The completed Pricing Schedule (Section 6) in the Excel template supplied. Do not alter the layout or formulae.
4. A copy of each insurance certificate and accreditation certificate referred to in Section 3.
5. The signed Form of Tender and Non-Collusion Declaration.

## 1.4 Format of responses

- Responses must be in Arial or Calibri, minimum 11 point, on A4 pages.
- Each response must be headed with the question number.
- Word limits are stated against each question. Words beyond a limit will not be read or scored. Headings, tables of figures and diagrams count towards the limit only where they contain prose.
- Do not include links to external websites. Material reached by a link will not be evaluated.
- Do not include marketing brochures. They will not be scored.

## 1.5 Clarification questions

All questions must be raised through the portal messaging function by the clarification deadline. The Council will publish questions and answers to all bidders without identifying the source. The Council will not answer questions received after the deadline unless the answer is needed to correct an error in the documents.

## 1.6 Site visits

One representative of each bidder must attend one site visit. The visit covers the Civic Centre, the Depot and a sample of public conveniences. Failure to attend will not exclude a bidder, but the Council will not accept later claims that site conditions were not understood.

## 1.7 Confidentiality and Freedom of Information

The Council is subject to the Freedom of Information Act 2000. Bidders should mark any information they consider commercially sensitive and say why. The Council may still be required to disclose it. Bidders must not publicise the procurement or the outcome without the Council's written consent.

## 1.8 Costs and variants

Each bidder bears its own costs of bidding. Variant bids are not permitted. The Council does not commit to award a contract and may abandon the procurement at any point.

## 1.9 Validity

Tenders must remain valid for 150 days from the submission deadline.

## 1.10 Subcontracting and consortia

Bidders must state in the Selection Questionnaire any proposed subcontractors who will deliver more than 10 percent of the contract value. Consortium bidders must nominate a lead entity that will be contractually responsible, and each member must complete a Selection Questionnaire.

# Section 2: Specification of Services

## 2.1 Purpose and outcomes

The Council requires a reliable, safe and good-value cleaning service that keeps its buildings presentable for residents, visitors and staff, keeps public conveniences open and hygienic, and supports the Council's climate and social value commitments. The Council expects the Contractor to deliver the outcomes below.

- Public-facing areas are clean and free of odour at the start of each opening period and remain so throughout the day.
- Public conveniences are available to the public during published opening hours, with faults and vandalism reported and rectified promptly.
- Staff and visitors are not exposed to avoidable risk from cleaning activity, chemicals or equipment.
- The Contractor's workforce is paid fairly, trained, supervised and treated with respect.

## 2.2 Premises

The premises are listed in full in Appendix A. In summary:

| Site | Type | Approx. floor area (m2) | Opening pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brackenhall Civic Centre | Council offices, committee rooms, public reception, council chamber | 4,800 | Mon to Fri 07:30 to 18:00; evening meetings up to 4 per month |
| Millbrook Depot offices and welfare block | Depot offices, mess room, shower and locker rooms | 650 | Mon to Sat 06:00 to 17:00 |
| Library and Customer Hub (Ferncombe) | Shared library and customer service point | 1,100 | Mon to Sat 09:00 to 17:30 |
| Brackenhall Register Office wing | Ceremony room, waiting area, offices | 520 | Mon to Sat, by appointment |
| 14 public conveniences | Mix of automated and traditional blocks | 15 to 60 each | Daily, 07:00 to 19:00 (summer), 08:00 to 17:00 (winter) |

Floor areas are indicative and bidders must satisfy themselves through the site visits.

## 2.3 Hours of service

Cleaning of offices and civic spaces will take place outside core occupancy where practicable: normally 17:00 to 21:00 on weekdays, with day porter provision at the Civic Centre between 07:30 and 15:30. Register Office ceremony rooms must be cleaned and presentable no later than 60 minutes before any scheduled ceremony. Public conveniences are cleaned on the rota in Section 2.5.

Periodic work will be programmed in advance and agreed with the Contract Manager.

## 2.5 Public conveniences

The Contractor will clean and inspect each public convenience at least once daily, and twice daily for the six highest-use sites identified in Appendix A. Each visit includes:

1. Inspection for damage, blockage, graffiti, needles or other hazards, reporting any hazard immediately to the Council's duty officer.
2. Cleaning and disinfection of all pans, urinals, basins, taps, hand dryers, fittings, walls and doors.
3. Mopping and disinfection of floors.
4. Restocking of paper, soap and sanitary disposal units.
5. Emptying of bins and sanitary waste containers.
6. Recording of the visit on the Council's digital check-in system or equivalent, with date and time.

Weekly, a deep clean of each convenience will be completed, including descaling, wall washing and attention to grout and drains. Where an automated unit is provided, the Contractor will follow the manufacturer's maintenance procedure. Reactive attendance is required within 3 hours (for hazards) or 24 hours (for non-hazardous incidents) of notification, including weekends and bank holidays.

## 2.6 Day porter service

A day porter is required at the Civic Centre on weekdays from 07:30 to 15:30. Duties include replenishing consumables, responding to spillages, cleaning public toilets, preparing committee rooms and chamber layouts for meetings, handling internal waste and recycling movement, and acting as a visible, courteous presence for visitors. Cover for absence must be arranged so the porter post is never vacant for a full day.

## 2.7 Standards

The Contractor will clean to the standards below.

- The cleaning standard for the contract will be the Association for Cleaning and Hygiene Excellence style "Cleaning Industry grade of cleanliness" approach, and the Council will use the APSE-style Cleanliness Index monitoring method (or equivalent) for audits. The target is a score of at least 85 percent on every audit.
- Staff must be trained to BICSc (British Institute of Cleaning Science) Level 2 or an equivalent standard within 3 months of starting work on the contract. Supervisors must hold BICSc Level 3 or equivalent.
- The Contractor must operate a documented quality management system, ideally certified to ISO 9001.
- Cleaning chemicals must be used in accordance with COSHH Regulations 2002 and the manufacturers' safety data sheets, which must be held on each site.
- Hygiene for washrooms and kitchens must follow industry good practice for infection prevention.

## 2.8 Consumables

The Contractor will supply cleaning materials, chemicals and equipment. Washroom consumables (toilet tissue, hand towels, soap, sanitary bags, bin liners) are supplied by the Contractor and charged at cost plus a management fee of no more than 5 percent, as set out in the Pricing Schedule. The Council reserves the right to specify products and to require recycled content of at least 80 percent for paper products.

## 2.9 Equipment and chemicals

Equipment must be electrically tested (PAT) and maintained, with records available on request. Battery or low-emission equipment is preferred. Microfibre systems with colour coding are required. Chemicals must be low-hazard where effective alternatives exist, and concentrates should be used with closed dosing systems. The Contractor will not use any substance on the Council's prohibited list, which will be issued at mobilisation.

## 2.10 Waste

The Contractor will segregate waste in line with the Council's recycling scheme (dry mixed recycling, food, glass, general waste) and will take sanitary waste only to the designated collection points. The Contractor will comply with the Waste (England and Wales) Regulations and Environmental Protection Act duty of care. Sharps found on site must not be handled except by staff trained and equipped to do so, and their locations must be reported at once.

## 2.11 Keys, security and access

The Contractor will hold keys and alarm codes securely, will keep a log of keyholders, and will report any loss within 2 hours. Staff must wear photographic identification and uniform at all times. All staff working in buildings with unsupervised access to Council offices must have a basic DBS certificate dated within the last 3 years.

## 2.12 Environmental requirements

The Contractor will reduce environmental impact by use of concentrates, reduced water usage, ecolabelled products where effective, and travel planning for supervisors and roaming staff. The Council expects reporting each year on chemical usage, waste volumes and vehicle miles.

## 2.13 Performance management

The Contractor will provide a named Contract Manager and Supervisors. The Council's Contract Manager will hold monthly review meetings and will carry out joint inspections at least monthly. Key performance indicators are below.

| KPI | Target | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| Audit cleanliness score | 85 percent or more at every audit | Monthly joint inspection |
| Public convenience visits completed on rota | 98 percent or more | Digital check-in data |
| Reactive call-out response (hazard) | Within 3 hours, 100 percent | Incident log |
| Complaints acknowledged | Within 1 working day, 100 percent | Complaints log |
| Staff turnover on contract | Below 20 percent per year | Contractor HR report |
| Mandatory training in date | 100 percent | Training matrix |

Service credits of 1 percent of the monthly charge apply for each audit below 80 percent, up to a maximum of 5 percent of the monthly charge, as set out in the draft contract.

## 2.14 Business continuity

The Contractor must hold a business continuity plan covering staff shortages, severe weather, fuel or supply interruption, pandemic and loss of premises, and must be able to maintain public convenience cleaning and Civic Centre critical areas at no less than 70 percent of the specified service during a major disruption.

## 2.15 Mobilisation

A mobilisation period of 10 weeks is allowed before service start. The Contractor will produce a mobilisation plan including TUPE consultation, DBS and right-to-work checks, uniform, equipment and chemical supply, induction, site familiarisation and a go-live checklist.

## 2.16 TUPE

The Council believes that the Transfer of Undertakings (Protection of Employment) Regulations 2006 (TUPE) will apply to the staff of the incumbent contractor who are assigned to this service. Anonymised employee liability information is in Appendix B and is provided in good faith. It is the bidder's responsibility to satisfy itself on its accuracy. In summary, 23 employees are listed, working a total of about 560 hours per week. The Council will supply updated information at least 28 days before the service start date. Bidders must price on the basis that TUPE applies. Pension arrangements for transferring staff are described in Appendix B.

## 2.17 Social value

The Council applies the Social Value Model aligned with national procurement policy. The Council's priorities are local employment and skills, tackling workforce inequality, and reducing carbon. Commitments made in the tender will be contractual and will be monitored annually.

# Section 4: Evaluation Methodology

## 4.1 Stages

1. Compliance check: tenders are checked for completeness and received on time.
2. Pass/fail: Section 3 requirements.
3. Quality and social value evaluation: Section 5 questions.
4. Price evaluation: Section 6.
5. Presentations to shortlisted bidders, which can confirm but not increase scores.
6. Award to the bidder with the highest combined score.

## 4.2 Weighting

| Criterion | Weighting |
|---|---|
| Quality (Section 5, questions 1.1 to 5.3) | 60 percent |
| Social value (Section 5, questions 6.1 and 6.2) | 10 percent |
| Price (Section 6) | 30 percent |
| Total | 100 percent |

The weighting shown against each question in Section 5 is its percentage of the total available score for the whole tender.

## 4.3 Scoring scale

Each quality and social value question is scored from 0 to 5 by a panel, then the score is converted to the question's weighting.

| Score | Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Unacceptable | No response, or response fails to address the question |
| 1 | Poor | Response addresses the question in a very limited way, with serious gaps or concerns |
| 2 | Weak | Response partly addresses the question with significant gaps and little evidence |
| 3 | Satisfactory | Response meets the requirement with adequate detail and some evidence |
| 4 | Good | Response meets the requirement in full with clear detail and relevant evidence |
| 5 | Excellent | Response exceeds the requirement, with detailed, credible, evidenced proposals and added value |

A score of 2 or below on any question marked with an asterisk (*) in Section 5 may lead to the bid being rejected.

Formula: question score = (panel score / 5) x question weighting.

## 4.4 Price evaluation

The lowest compliant total price over the initial term, calculated from the Pricing Schedule, receives the full 30 points. Other bids receive: 30 x (lowest price / bidder's price). Abnormally low tenders will be investigated and may be rejected.

## 4.5 Moderation

Evaluators will score individually then meet to agree a consensus score. A record of the rationale will be kept and made available in debrief.

# Section 6: Pricing Schedule

## 6.1 Basis of pricing

Bidders must complete the Excel Pricing Schedule. All prices must be in pounds sterling, exclusive of VAT. Prices are fixed for the first 12 months. After that, labour elements will be uplifted by the percentage increase in the Real Living Wage (or National Living Wage if higher), and non-labour elements by the Consumer Prices Index for the preceding September, capped at 4 percent.

## 6.2 Components

1. Fixed monthly charge for the cleaning of each site listed in Appendix A, covering all frequencies in Section 2.4 and the day porter service.
2. Fixed monthly charge for public convenience cleaning, per site, covering daily and weekly work.
3. Schedule of periodic work rates (carpet extraction, external windows, high-level cleaning), per m2 or per visit.
4. Day rates for additional operatives and supervisors for ad hoc work, with weekday, evening, weekend and bank holiday rates.
5. Reactive call-out rates, within and outside normal hours.
6. Management fee percentage on consumables, which must not exceed 5 percent.
7. Mobilisation costs, shown separately and amortised in the monthly charge only if the bidder states so.

## 6.3 Price evaluation sample

For evaluation, the Council will use a total price made up of:

| Element | Evaluation basis |
|---|---|
| Fixed monthly charges, 36 months | Sum of monthly charges |
| Estimated periodic work | Pre-set quantities in the template |
| Ad hoc hours | 400 operative hours and 80 supervisor hours per year at the rates quoted |
| Consumables | Council estimated spend of £38,000 per year plus the management fee |
| Reactive call-outs | 30 per year at the rate quoted |

## 6.4 Assumptions

Bidders must state any assumptions. Assumptions that qualify the Council's requirements may lead to the tender being rejected. Bidders must show the hourly pay rate, employer on-costs and overhead and profit percentages assumed in a build-up of price, to be used to test for abnormally low tenders.

## 6.5 Payment

The Council pays undisputed invoices within 30 days of receipt. Invoices are monthly in arrears.
